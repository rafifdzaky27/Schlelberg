## Per-chapter working packet

Load this compact canon, the current chapter card, the previous chapter's last scene and state entry, any intervening other-POV chapter's state, and relevant rule/profile excerpts. For a shield fight load combat rules; for captivity load prisoner arithmetic; for a reveal load its ledger row. If uncertain, read the relevant full file rather than guess. Economy must not become a rule to ignore important context.

1. **Prepare:** identify the entry state, three scene turns, information limits, and the chapter's new irreversible change. Note facts that must remain unresolved.
2. **Draft:** write complete scenes in third-person close past tense. Scene lengths can vary; allocate more space to the emotional/tactical turn than to transit. Targets are approximate, not quotas.
3. **Read as prose:** check spatial clarity, voice, actual desire, a changed relationship, and whether the chapter is interesting beyond compliance with the card. An outline can be mechanically fulfilled and still dull.
4. **Audit locally:** check timeline, bodies, objects, count, knowledge, rule use, and next chapter's required entry. Remove duplicated explanation and ornamental aphorisms.
5. **Record:** save the revised chapter and a 150–250-word state entry. Mark which planned setups were actually written. Do not mark a setup complete because it appears in an outline.
6. **Report briefly:** Indonesian update, no automatic full-chapter dump. Ask a choice only if it changes architecture or materially improves the author's intended experience.

Draft in one- or two-chapter batches unless the author requests a larger run. At movement boundaries (5, 12, 20, 25, 32), audit the movement as a whole once. Avoid rereading every completed page after each minor sentence edit. After Chapter 32, perform the full manuscript pass and assemble the novel only after resolving real findings.

## Style and tone

**Narration:** clear, concrete, emotionally attentive. Begin from what the viewpoint character needs or notices, then widen when the view changes their understanding. The narrator may be more rhythmically articulate than spoken dialogue, but cannot import technical knowledge the character lacks. One head per chapter. Avoid omniscient hints such as 'he did not yet know how terribly he would regret it.'

**World introduction:** put a feature under pressure. A seized measure teaches politics; a stalled transit system teaches city maintenance; a shield dropping during a cut teaches combat. Beauty also deserves space without every view concealing corruption. Veyr must be somewhere Tobious might want to live, and Aras somewhere Mariya actually wants, not a miserable place she remains in out of obligation.

**Epic register:** scale comes from visible relationships among small people, monumental spaces, long time, and consequences. Prefer a specific line of evacuees beneath a city-sized structure to repeated assertions that worlds tremble. Keep encounters spatially readable: objective, positions, obstacle, attempted action, changed position, cost. Do not make action incomprehensible to simulate speed.

**Poetry:** concentrate it around an earned observation. One strong sentence can serve a chapter; none is required. Avoid stacked metaphors, constant personified silence, repeated sentence fragments, 'not X but Y' epigrams, and every conversation ending with a maxim. A memorable line should be something the scene has made newly true.

**Dialogue:** Tobious asks concretely and becomes terse when ashamed; Mariya counts obligations and can be dry; Ypin specifies limits and dislikes loose claims; Panjios teaches through observed mistakes, sometimes evasively; Suss offers operationally persuasive arguments; Edda asks what can be verified and whom a plan will actually treat. Do not make Mariya always right or Ypin always funny. Let a person answer a question with behavior when speech would overexplain.

**Romance:** attraction is established familiarity plus recognition of changing competence, not rescue debt. Include touch, shared pleasures, annoyance, embarrassment, and the comfort of known habits. A disagreement need not threaten all affection. No love triangle, jealous subordinate, or sexually violent villain scene as a cheap intensifier. Ch29's separation must hurt because a shared life would have been desirable, not because the narrator announces destiny.

**Grief:** allow missing habits, exhaustion, irritability, interrupted tasks, and care by others. Don't describe a diagnosis as a personality. Panjios's loss is not cured by killing Suss. Survival after injury can involve adaptation and pleasure as well as loss.

**Names and language:** the Iwang / an Iwang / two Iwang; the Hollow Ones / a Hollow One. Capitalize Concord, Wardens when institutional, Severance, Unbroken Edge, Root Hall, Salt Gate, Quarry Gate. 'Schlelberg' names the runaway overlap, not a god or nation. Keep plain English without pseudo-archaic 'thou,' modern internet slang, or pages of transliterated invented vocabulary.

## Chapter acceptance checks

- Does someone want something specific, face resistance, change tactics, and leave with an altered situation?
- Does the chapter contribute an experience beyond information transfer: fear, pleasure, tenderness, shame, wonder, or difficult understanding?
- Can the reader follow where people stand and why the chosen action changes the problem?
- Is knowledge limited to the viewpoint and believable communication? Have any rules been invented merely to save the scene?
- Are dates, count, injuries, travel, and object ownership consistent with the ledger?
- Are affection and dissent both possible, or has dialogue become an editorial debate with character names attached?
- Is a phrase memorable because of its scene, or merely trying to sound quotable?
- Does the ending create a meaningful next pressure? A cliffhanger is optional; a changed state is not.
