# STORY CONTRACT

## Premise and reader promise

A farmer in an occupied Bronze Age village opens a buried threshold and sees an injured version of himself in a collapsing future city. Recruited because the threshold has bound itself to his nervous system, he learns to wield a legendary blade that can sever the connections between eras. When an attempted rescue traps Maria and other villagers in an Iwang nest, he accepts a faster, more dangerous method from the commander training him. His mistake exposes the future to an incursion and costs his other mentor his life. He must then build a rescue with people he has treated as recipients of his decisions, stop a policy that counts the past as expendable, and close the only connection between their worlds. The successful closure leaves him in the future and Maria in the life she chooses to rebuild in the past.

Promise: tactile occupied-village life, unsettling temporal wonder, beautiful advanced infrastructure with visible maintenance and inequality, learnable shield-and-blade combat, nonhuman monsters with coherent behavior, a romance between two deciding adults, a compromised mentor, and a victory achieved through collective judgment rather than greater damage output.

## What the novel argues through action

Question: does the ability to save people give you the right to choose what they lose?

Toby's opening answer: if he becomes strong enough, he can protect people and prevent them from having to choose losses. His developed answer: power makes him responsible for what he does, not the owner of other people's lives. Good leadership includes disclosure, consent under pressure, delegation, and accepting that people may choose a life different from the one he wants for them.

Counterargument embodied by Sus: in an emergency, uncoordinated freedom kills; a leader must assign costs to others to secure the larger system. Sus is sometimes correct about urgency and coordination. He becomes the antagonist because he excludes the ancient population from moral and political standing, suppresses alternatives, and kills to preserve his authority over the calculation.

Panji's partial answer: restraint matters, but shame and secrecy can make compassionate leadership paternalistic. His last action rectifies one immediate harm; it does not erase the people he abandoned twelve years earlier.

Maria's answer: being loved is not consent to be removed from one's community. Her flaw is treating herself as an endlessly renewable resource. She must delegate and admit uncertainty rather than hold her group together through promises she cannot guarantee.

Ipin's answer: truth has practical value when it changes what happens to people. He must stop treating the preservation of proof as an automatic priority over rescue.

## What is preserved, rebuilt, removed, new

Preserved: farmer protagonist, his actual future voice, two eras, Maria, Panji, Sus, Iwang, dangerous power, mentor death, growth toward leadership, an epic blade, and a surviving group after conflict.

Rebuilt: training becomes timed practice plus operations; corruption becomes a knowingly accepted operating method and moral compromise, not an evil sword controlling every decision. Maria acts inside captivity. Sus's betrayal has strategic purpose. Panji's death follows Toby's and Panji's choices. The ending resolves this novel's threat.

Removed: repeated anonymous mentors, Aleron as an additional guide, the ungrounded 'you must sacrifice love' prophecy, random future trips, vague psychic tests, instant skill downloads, Toppan/Melissa turning into Rafiq/Lara, a new omniscient entity appearing in the ending, literal recruitment of everyone into a species called Toby.

New: occupation and grain administration, Aras/Veyr's shared terrain, Ipin's maintenance/data function, Eda as an independent clinician, finite portal sites, the Schlelberg overlap process, personhood classification fraud, a rigorously limited inception echo, and a planned evacuation that requires a permanent closure.

The formal organization is **the Concord**, its field operatives **Wardens**. Toby remains a person's name. 'Leader' may appear as praise or recruitment rhetoric, not as a magic class or a title all characters inexplicably know. This is an intentional reconstruction decision: the original plural-Toby idea becomes shared wardenship, while the literal future self is preserved in the echo.

## Five movements and irreversible turns

### I. Occupation and departure, Chapters 1–5

Toby fails against a trained soldier while defending seed grain. Forced labor uncovers the threshold. The first opening is an image/sound echo; the subsequent live connection allows an Iwang through. Toby helps shut it using worksite knowledge, injuring the local system and becoming its living reference. Panji arrives because the monitoring system detects activation, not by narrative coincidence. A real extraction bargain supplies medicine and grain to Aras. Toby goes to learn enough to return and protect people; he does not abandon Maria without telling her.

First commitment: crossing to Veyr, accepting supervised calibration and training, and promising an honest return attempt rather than guaranteed rescue.

### II. Instruction and evidence, Chapters 6–12

Future medicine helps but does not erase injuries. Sus demonstrates lifesaving competence. Toby learns that survival in shield combat depends on timing and partners. The blade accepts his acquired field reference; publicity makes this look like a moral election. Ipin discovers that village populations have been counted as maintenance burden rather than protected people. Maria independently collects labor tallies and tests the new supplies. Their observations converge before anyone announces a conspiracy.

Turn: Toby joins a sanctioned return operation with evidence that its manifest conceals people. He now has reason to distrust orders, but not enough knowledge to treat all caution as cowardice.

### III. Intervention and temptation, Chapters 13–20

Toby prematurely severs a live transfer branch to release twelve workers. The choice succeeds locally and strands a second group, including Maria, in the Receiver enclosure. Forty-eight civilians become captive anchors. Panji orders withdrawal to prevent immediate expansion; Toby obeys while hating the necessity. Maria builds survival and escape arrangements. Ipin finds Panji's older abandonment order and the current classification trick. Panji admits his signature. Sus offers faster unguarded cuts and a seemingly workable selective rescue. Toby accepts expanded operation after a successful, genuinely helpful trial. Captives then experience costs that the training display failed to represent.

Midpoint transformation: Toby stops trying merely to qualify as a Warden and begins challenging the system; his first independent intervention also gives him personal culpability. Knowledge alone does not make his next choice wise.

### IV. Failure and responsibility, Chapters 21–25

Sus uses the expanded connection to seize decisive control. Toby initially follows the plan he accepted, then refuses the commanded sacrifice. Panji holds an isolation shutter while Toby and Ipin get people through. Sus kills Panji to prevent him completing shutdown. Toby escapes by Ipin's previously established maintenance route, not because Sus forgets to pursue. Official access is revoked; Sus publicly blames the incursion on Toby's unauthorized behavior.

Toby nearly surrenders his role. He considers handing back the sword and accepting Sus's isolation of the past. His recovery is several days of limited useful work, testimony from Maria, verification of alternative calculations, and a chosen obligation—not one inspirational speech. Eda supports the alternative only after reviewing concrete casualty and infrastructure plans. Toby discloses his errors to collaborators and lets them alter his plan.

Final commitment: evacuate the captives, prepare future services for disconnection, and permanently sever the root. Toby learns before launch that the last operator cannot cross afterward. He does not conceal this fact from Maria or Ipin.

### V. Rescue and separation, Chapters 26–32

Maria organizes the captive side of the escape. Toby returns to Aras with a shield and Severance but resists the temptation to settle every occupation grievance by force. Two of the remaining forty-five prisoners die during the dangerous evacuation despite meaningful attempts to save them; forty-three reach safety. The rescue does not wait for Sus's defeat.

Toby and Maria explicitly consider crossing together. She chooses to remain in her own era, not because only a woman can perform care or because a new magical restriction forbids her crossing. Her home, work, and commitments are part of the life she wants. Toby's task requires him to close the root from the future side. Their love is acknowledged and reciprocated; neither regards the other's decision as betrayal.

Sus attempts a one-sided purge that preserves Veyr's infrastructure while destroying the ancient node and its inhabited surroundings. Toby, Ipin, and Eda's authorized emergency personnel disable this sequence. Sus is disarmed and taken alive. Toby does not win a fair master-swordsman duel: he survives long enough to execute a team plan and gives up a killing opportunity to protect a rescue route.

In the final root overload, the inception echo connects Toby to Chapter 1 for forty-one seconds. His shorter hair, left-eye scar, right-rib wound, speech, and the failing skyway match. He then completes the root cut. The mineral reference is destroyed; Severance's active core is spent. All three inter-era sites cease permanently. Shield technology and ordinary future infrastructure remain technically possible; rerouting and shortages continue.

Toby survives with a lasting right-hand deficit. Ipin stays as a friend and collaborator, not a replacement romantic partner. Evidence sufficient for an inquiry survives even though Ipin abandoned the most comprehensive cache during rescue. Panji is remembered with his culpability intact. Maria's final chapter shows surviving Aras households sowing the seed they preserved, negotiating practical autonomy from a weakened garrison, and refusing to substitute a hero cult for communal decisions. The book closes its rescue, monster breach, institutional exposure, romance, and identity promises. No last-page larger villain.

## Why Toby must remain in the future

The root's material control seam is physically in Veyr. Severance must be in contact and receive continuous living feedback from the bound reference for the final cut. There is no remote or timed activation. A replacement reference requires seven uninterrupted days with every inter-era branch idle; the captives' anchor chamber cannot be idled without killing them and releasing the nest. After their release, convergence leaves hours, not a week. The existence of this transfer method and its conditions is established early, not revealed during the goodbye.

Once the root is cut, all local receiver routes fail together. Closing one gate while leaving another is not a solution: the endangered volume is one lattice with three access sites. The material sustaining the chronological offset is consumed; no spare root exists for these eras. There is no independent portal to the same past elsewhere.

Maria could elect to settle in Veyr before the closure. She chooses not to. Do not write a false physical prohibition merely to force the agreed ending. The machinery limits Toby; the relationship respects Maria's agency.

## Main causal chain

Telassar occupies a productive harbor → future-linked prospectors exploit the occupation to excavate a buried receiver → forced labor puts Toby at its damaged contact → he becomes the reference and sees the echo → reopening draws an Iwang → damage forces intervention by Wardens → Panji needs Toby's consent and skill development → Toby sees both future competence and exclusion → evidence from Maria and Ipin prompts intervention → premature severance saves one group but strands another → urgency and distrust of Panji make Sus's shortcut attractive → amplified operation enables a breach and Sus's seizure of control → Toby's refusal forces a retreat Panji holds open at fatal cost → grief and guilt almost end Toby's participation → verified alternatives and other people's choices restore limited responsibility → a joint rescue removes the captives before the root decision → Sus loses leverage and attempts a purge → team action stops him → final severance fulfills the future-self echo and separates the eras → survivors must build lives without undoing the cost.
