# WORLD BIBLE AND NON-NEGOTIABLE OPERATING RULES

## 1. Time and the two societies

Planning dates: ancient **Year 718**, future **Year 3318**, in a fictional continuous coastal chronology. Exactly 2,600 years separate paired sites. Do not identify either with Gregorian years or real Greek history. The opening need not print a date. Establish the elapsed centuries in Chapter 6 through a comprehensible demonstration, not a lecture or Toby instantly reading unfamiliar digits.

One history exists. The inhabitants cannot overwrite established events, create replacement selves, or reverse deaths. Regular gates pair an ancient date with the corresponding future date 2,600 years later. A day lived on one side is a day elapsed at the paired endpoint. There are no training-time discounts, movable destination dates, or arbitrary stopovers in other centuries.

Future historical records are fragmentary. False manifests and selective classifications, not a mutable universe, explain the apparent contradiction between recorded 'no protected inhabitants' and actual captives. Actions in Aras are part of the history that produced Veyr. Do not show Veyr changing retroactively when someone moves a stone or decides differently in Aras.

The locals' years and the future's dates are reconciled by Concord archaeology; ancient peasants generally count harvests and reigns. Both names for years belong in records, not constant narration.

## 2. Geography: one landscape seen across centuries

**Aras:** approximately eighty households on terraces overlooking a narrow bay. Mud-brick upper walls on stone feet; shared granary; threshing floor; olive groves above, barley and pulses below; boatbuilding yard at the shore; narrow lanes; a communal well and ancestor terrace over much older masonry. No marble metropolis or uniform fantasy tavern district. Houses show maintenance, repaired baskets, soot, stored water, and people still trying to work after invasion.

**Telassari citadel:** ridge across the valley, ninety minutes' ordinary walking from Aras; occupied former local palace. It commands the road and harbor levy. Telassar is a native expansionist palace kingdom, not a disguised future civilization. Its ruler wants grain, labor, and access to the bay. The conquest is real politics; not every soldier knows anything about temporal technology.

**Old Storehouse / Receiver:** under the ancestor terrace; ordinary upper granary is a different building. Entry through stairs, a passage, a circular flooded chamber, then lower steps and a black threshold. Preserve these relative positions. Old drains run toward the bay. The central receiver and connected annex become the captive enclosure after Chapter 13. The inhabitants are phase-tethered but still located on the ancient side; they are not in a time-stopped pocket universe.

**Salt Gate:** auxiliary receiver in abandoned salt storage near the shore, forty minutes downhill from Aras, twenty minutes farther along the coast to its concealed mouth. Used for small scheduled crossings and supply. Do not invent an ocean voyage for this walk.

**Quarry Gate:** emergency auxiliary in inland stone workings, three hours uphill from Aras, connected to the same root. Useful for field access, not a loophole after closure. Closed by damage during the failed operation; parts of its service channel remain usable for information and planned evacuation.

**Veyr:** the future city occupying the valley and cliffs above the old bay. Shoreline engineering and centuries of rebuilding obscure ancient alignments. The citadel ridge is now the foundation of the Civic Spine. Layered walkways span open air; transit runs in guided channels; sun-control surfaces shade housing; food grows in controlled towers and reservoirs support civic cooling. These systems have operators and repair access. Wealthy districts experience them as effortless; service districts live beside heat, noise, and ration interruptions.

**Root Hall:** original field root beneath the Civic Spine, containing the physical seam Severance must cut. The Wardens' training rooms, memorial gallery, and Concord control offices are nearby but distinct. Access is controlled by credentials and human guards, not the sword acting as a universal door key.

**Lower Service Ward:** Ipin's work district, Eda's clinic, manual reservoirs, retired maintenance walks. It becomes a place of refuge because Ipin knows its people and routes before the crisis. It is not magically invisible to authorities; fugitives move cautiously, receive limited help, and face searches.

**High Concourse:** transit/structural junction above Root Hall. The skyway and skyline seen in Chapter 1 appear here in Chapter 31. Write the later view from the reverse position with the same road segment and light conditions.

## 3. Ancient life, economy, and occupation

Bronze Age Aegean inspiration: barley, legumes, figs, olives, wool, linen, pottery, bronze blades/tools, stone weights, seals, clay administrative records, pack animals, palatial storage, and large older foundations. Grain and labor obligations dominate; do not introduce coin wages casually. Common farm tools include wood and stone as well as scarce bronze fittings. Soldiers' equipment differs by rank. There are no classical Spartan institutions, Roman legions, medieval steel plate, or generic ancient democracy.

The king's altered measure makes domination concrete. Twelve of fourteen seed jars are seized in Chapter 1, two officially reserved. They are a specific shared seed reserve, not the entirety of eighty households' food. Other household stores and garden crops exist but are being depleted. One remaining jar will be distributed during acute hunger; one is preserved for the final sowing. Record this, rather than producing unlimited hidden grain.

Occupation includes forced repair, detained laborers, controlled exits, transport levies, and confiscated sacred property. It also includes native cooperation, bargaining, soldiers who follow ordinary incentives, and disagreements about resistance. Do not make every Telassari a sadist or every villager united and brave. The officer's insistence on a usable laborer explains why Toby is punished with work rather than executed in Chapter 1.

Aras has customary household meetings and shared storage, not universal modern parliamentary institutions. Maria's final authority comes from recognizable work and surviving people's consent. The political ending wins a seasonal relief/withdrawal arrangement and practical local control, not the magical disappearance of Telassar.

The woman-with-bowl statue and first-water offering are invented local practice. Removal wounds communal continuity. No actual goddess needs to manifest or reveal the sword. Religion offers rituals of grief and obligation but does not supply a last-minute solution.

Language: the prose translates both societies into readable English. Panji has learned the ancient regional language through field work. Future interpretation devices help Toby understand routine speech but do not instantly teach him technical literacy or make him literate in old palace script. Ipin occasionally has to restate a technical phrase; do not make this a recurring joke at Toby's expense.

## 4. Future society and institutions

The Concord maintains the inter-era lattice and trains Wardens. It is an institution with public responsibilities, competing departments, and real competence. It is not a uniformly evil cult. 'Truth' is part of its legitimating rhetoric: verified records are treated as a basis for authority, which allows classification fraud to matter.

Sus commands field operations and can invoke emergency access. Panji controls training/calibration and has old authority but cannot freely overrule every operational order. Ipin is a maintenance and manifest technician with bounded permissions; Eda is an independent clinic physician with civil-emergency authority and patients whose testimony matters.

A technical resource label, **unregistered biological load**, is used for ancient people outside protected-person counts. A survey can truthfully report zero protected residents while concealing dozens of humans. The distinction must be made legible with two actual columns and a name the reader knows. Do not hide behind pages of jargon.

Medicine: disinfecting, imaging, wound sealing, splinting, and assisted rehabilitation are advanced. Nerves, blood loss, deep structural damage, and infection still impose recovery. No resurrection, instant regeneration, or downloading combat skills. Toby can remain sore, angry, dependent, and impatient without portraying care as weakness.

Communication/transit: reliable within ordinary Veyr coverage, interrupted by thick service masonry and active lattice distortion. Fixed paired service relays can transmit narrow-band audio/text during powered diagnostic windows. There is no live omniscient view of captives. Survey wafers can record and buffer messages. They cannot open gates, locate everyone, or supply unlimited power. Specific relay openings are logged in the timeline.

Military logic: this novel depicts fighting around vulnerable urban infrastructure, confined receivers, and captives. Precision capture and preservation matter. Heavy demolition exists and is dangerous; shields do not make bombardment physically impossible. Its use around the root risks Schlelberg and is an unacceptable substitute for rescue. Do not claim that all warfare everywhere has become honorable dueling.

## 5. The lattice and Schlelberg

The paired system consists of one root in Veyr and three receiver accesses in the ancient volume: Storehouse, Salt, Quarry. All share one irreplaceable mineral phase-reference. The root is a relic of an earlier technically capable culture, usually called the First Makers; present engineers can service interfaces but cannot recreate the phase-reference. Ancient builders later enclosed surviving structures in the masonry under Aras. This history is background, not a third active era or an unanswered new cosmic villain.

Schlelberg is the name for **runaway overlap between the paired inhabited volumes**. The damaged lattice begins stabilizing its load by drawing on living nervous systems; Iwang exploit the gradients and establish nests. Increasing overlap produces displaced surfaces, shared pressure effects, failed doors, and future service surges. Do not describe it as a mood, an unseen dark king, or a virus with whatever effects a scene needs.

The measured convergence deadline is Day 84 after Chapter 1. Early estimates are uncertain; by Chapter 17, independent observations agree on the operating window. This is an engineering deadline caused by accumulated stress and predictable load, not an arbitrary celestial prophecy. Sus's unauthorized gain shortens local safety margins but does not move the final deadline differently in each chapter.

There are three strategies: (a) continue using captive anchors, extending suffering and losing control; (b) Sus's terminal purge destroys the ancient receiver volume while protecting the future root and its useful infrastructure; (c) evacuate first, prepare future services to disconnect, then destroy the root reference and all temporal access permanently. Strategy (c) is difficult, not a painless clever third door. People die during rescue; Veyr suffers damage, rationing, and displacement.

Why not evacuate Aras earlier? Before Chapter 13, general resettlement is refused by Concord policy and native occupation control, not falsely presented as a universal law of physics. Panji can negotiate supplies and exceptional patients, proving the restriction is political. After captivity begins, people are tethered inside a guarded receiver enclosure; removal requires sequential release and safe routes. This change must be explicit.

## 6. The inception echo: the only backward information exception

The first contact at the damaged Storehouse interface acquires Toby as the living reference. Root closure on Day 84 generates a single **forty-one-second sensory echo** back to that reference's initial acquisition. It is not a selectable communications service. Images and sound pass; bodies, air, weapons, and heat do not. Chapter 2 establishes this with a physical test. The water going still and the lamp flame deforming in Chapter 1 are local field effects, not wind from the future.

After the echo ends, the still-powered receiver can open an ordinary physical route to the paired current future date. The first monster enters through this second state, not through the echo. Distinguish their visible behavior in Chapter 2.

There is no later replay from the past and no continuous prediction. The inception words were always those spoken at closure; Toby cannot edit them into a different history. Future logs show a diagnostic anomaly at Day 0 but not a complete recording of the Day-84 conversation. Nobody has an omniscient film of the plot. Explain enough by Chapters 12 and 24 that the final recognition is earned.

The existing opening's final accusation is revised: 'They aren't looking for stores. That door is what they came for. And now they know you can open it.' This preserves menace without implying Sus or the army knew Toby by name before his acquisition. The echo man is Toby after twelve weeks of injury and change, not decades older. His apparent age is an impression, consistent with Chapter 1.

## 7. Living reference and why the sword accepts Toby

The interface's normal synthetic regulator is broken. During direct conductive contact, it acquires the first viable nervous system as a temporary reference. Blood is incidental conductive contact; no hereditary blood test or chosen royal lineage. The overseer did not know which worker would be acquired. Direct contact makes Toby locally compatible, not globally strongest or morally chosen.

Transferring the reference is possible only after seven uninterrupted days of idle receivers, with old and new references present for supervised calibration. Establish this in Chapter 9 through procedure, and revisit it when Toby asks to withdraw. The tethered prisoners prevent an idle week during captivity. After evacuation, the terminal window is too short. No volunteer swap at the last minute.

A reference is necessary for active severance but does not give telepathy, foresight, universal healing, command over monsters, or automatic fighting skill. His body experiences accumulated strain. Medical personnel can measure it; ideology may interpret it as destiny. The narrative must keep those apart.

## 8. Shields and combat

Personal shields spread and redirect sudden incoming impulse. Ordinary thrown/fired projectiles and simple energy strikes are inefficient against intact units. A powered blade uses sustained near-contact feedback to couple through one small sector. Detached projectiles cannot maintain this operator-controlled coupling. Exotic demolition tools can attack shields, but are not portable miracle pistols introduced to invalidate established combat.

Canonical rhythm: acquire blade contact/near-contact for roughly three heartbeats; engage penetration; the attacker's own full shield drops during the coupled strike; recover and restore the shield. Training teaches distance, feints, control of the opposing blade, protection by partners, and retreat. Highly skilled fighters can shorten the acquisition through preparation, not ignore it entirely. A novice cannot win against Sus by becoming angry.

Shields distribute momentum but do not remove gravity, bad footing, suffocation, falls, exhaustion, or every crushing load. A shove can move the wearer. Units have heat and charge limits. A quarter-hour of light use is not the same as repeated heavy impacts; show warning states rather than arbitrary percentages. An ordinary unit can be serviced from standard city power; temporal root closure does not abolish every shield in Veyr.

Ancient soldiers have bronze weapons and hide shields, not secret personal fields. A future-shielded Toby has a major advantage against them, which creates an ethical temptation; he still cannot stand indefinitely against a crowd or rescue people by recklessly triggering feedback near the root.

## 9. Severance, the Unbroken Edge

Official proper name **Severance**; old ceremonial epithet **the Unbroken Edge**. Physical object: a restrained, pale metal blade with a narrow dark channel, a plain grip repaired by earlier hands, and a small opaque core in the pommel. No floating interface, endless colored beams, cartoon enlargement, or sword speaking as an extra mentor. Preserve its recognizable silhouette.

It is the last serviceable root-cutting key from the First Makers, celebrated because an earlier bearer ended an incursion at personal cost. Later propaganda turns access into legitimacy. The legend contains a real deed but not proof that every bearer is good. Its rarity follows the irreplaceable core material, not convenient refusal to manufacture ordinary swords.

Modes: ordinary blade (no superhuman skill); guarded field cut (local connection only, approximately three heartbeats of coupling, shield down, then at least thirty seconds for the grip/core to settle); unguarded cut (Sus's bypass suppresses the recovery interlock, can repeat more quickly but stores load in the carrier and connected receiver bodies); terminal root severance (continuous contact and living feedback for fifteen heartbeats; consumes the reference and active core permanently).

The guarded mode cannot free an entire nest with one swing. Prisoner tethers must be isolated sequentially and the exit route kept stable. The bypass genuinely saves time in controlled tests. The lie is the incomplete accounting of who absorbs its cost in a live network, not that it never works.

The sword does not steal souls, judge virtue, fix injuries, open unrelated locks, cut any concept, or grant a new mode in the finale. At the end the inert physical blade remains broken/spent. It can be preserved as evidence, never restored into a back door home.

## 10. The Iwang / the Hollow Ones

Nonhuman organisms adapted to the interstitial habitat around phase gradients. They were never secretly villagers or future Toby clones. Individuality exists through behavior, not humanoid villain speeches. The nickname comes from visible open spaces in their torsos and the sound they produce when air passes through those cavities; it does not assert the absence of souls.

Baseline form: tall, articulated bodies, hard pale external struts, dark tissues around a collar/throat nexus, flexible gripping limbs. Their silhouette is recognizable. They can flatten through an already thinned boundary near a live gradient, not walk through any intact wall at will. Attack requires local coherence, exposing the collar nexus. This provides a learnable but dangerous opening for physical weapons.

Signals: mineral clicking, a pressure change across a nearby shield, dust/water responding to a gradient before the body appears. Use a limited repeatable vocabulary. Do not give each appearance a new sensory power. They do not mimic loved ones' voices or read memories in this version.

They preserve captive humans as living stabilizers and take field energy, not ordinary meals from them. Humans still need water, food, sanitation, and movement. Daran's handlers supply minimal rations under Sus's concealment scheme to keep the anchors alive; the monsters are not inexplicably feeding people. Maria's organization and captured stores matter when that supply falters.

Sus directs routes with energized baffles and can temporarily keep a work corridor unattractive to the Iwang. He cannot command their minds. Removing baffles can expose his own people. Monsters can die, withdraw, adapt to repeated feints, and be driven back by root closure. The ending ends their access to these inhabited volumes, not the species throughout existence.

## 11. Daily life that makes the worlds worth saving

Aras has communal labor without perfect communal harmony. Households contest irrigation turns, borrow animals, remember debts in goods and labor, and argue over whose roof should be repaired first. A child's coming-of-age is marked by a first full share of harvest work, not a universal warrior initiation. Marriage and households can be negotiated through kin and property, but Toby and Maria are unmarried adults able to choose their relationship. Avoid casually imposing a specific historical Greek law on this invented society.

Education is practical and unequal: children learn tasks, songs, local routes, and household measures; administrative literacy belongs largely to palace workers and some storekeepers. Maria's limited tally literacy gives her leverage without making her a modern accountant in costume. Toby can recognize his household mark and ordinary quantities without reading every document. Occupation law is enforced through collective levies, detention, household liability, and access to land. Customary negotiation survives beneath imposed authority.

Future education provides broad basic literacy and highly specialized training. Most citizens can use infrastructure they cannot rebuild. This gap matters: Ipin understands service interfaces but not lost reference synthesis. Public transit and routine care are normal expectations; registration determines access to some protections and emergency priority. News travels through civic displays and personal devices, with command notices identified by issuer. Sus can publish an official emergency claim quickly; he cannot delete every independent witness or media channel. Eda's eventual case works by authenticated evidence, corroboration, and civic authority, not one viral post that instantly ends all resistance.

Food in Veyr includes cultivated greens, grain grown in controlled beds, fermented protein, and household preparation. Imported luxuries exist but do not need a trade atlas. Leisure includes shared cooking, open-air viewing terraces, music transmitted through public sound surfaces at agreed hours, and neighborhood repair games. Ipin enjoys taking apart harmless objects and is occasionally bad at putting a domestic one back together; Toby enjoys making food with his hands and recognizes weather that the city tries to regulate. Their friendship needs a pleasure the plot could momentarily leave alone.

Future wealth is visible as quiet, space, reliable shade, and distance from service heat; poverty is interruption, crowding, and having to negotiate a repair. This is more specific than rich people wearing black and poor people wearing gray. Neither era is morally pure. Advanced medicine is a genuine good; ancient communities can be coercive or exclusionary. Choosing Aras is Maria's choice of a life, not the novel proving modernity inherently false.

Do not use every sensory column in every visit. Choose the two details the current viewpoint needs, plus one unexpected but credible pleasure or discomfort. Toby initially perceives future engineering by function and analogy; his vocabulary becomes more exact after teaching. Maria notices the labor required to keep a beautiful place usable.

## 13. Epic scale without constant catastrophe

Use six distinct visual peaks: the occupied grain court; the impossible city beyond cold ankle-deep water; Toby's first full view of Veyr above the changed bay; Severance's restrained first live coupling against a dark barrier; a line of people moving beneath the pale Iwang through the old masonry; and the failing skyway viewed from the other end of the opening image. Each has a human objective and a cost. Between them, let meals, work, disagreement, desire, and landscape restore scale.

The sword's heroic weight comes from history, public expectation, recognizable appearance, and the bearer having to remain exposed while others cover him. Give its gallery scene ceremonial space and silence, but do not reveal an ancient monarchy or copy a sword-in-stone test. Shield combat emphasizes close contact, coordinated vulnerability, and infrastructure constraints. Avoid importing Dune's specific mythology, terminology, factions, or iconic combat explanation verbatim.

Schlelberg grows through three visible stages: early local boundary anomalies (Ch1–12); linked captivity and service surges (Ch13–23); city-scale structural overlap and threatened ancient-volume purge (Ch24–31). Keep the Iwang frightening at each scale through learned limitations being hard to exploit under pressure, not new arbitrary powers.

## 14. Operational clarifications for the final rescue

After the Chapter-57 overload, a forecast low-load trough on Days 82–83 is the earliest safe opportunity for mass untethering. Seed the changing load pattern in Chapter 17; calculate the usable trough with Maria's observed baffle intervals in Chapter 24. The buildup culminates on Day 84. Thus preparation time is necessary, and only hours remain for root closure after evacuation. Selective extraction earlier is possible at redistributed cost, explaining Sus's offer without permitting an effortless general rescue. No new cosmic alignment governs the dates.

The freed Iwang do not become unlimited animals loose forever in ordinary space. Outside an active gradient they lose coherence within minutes and retreat or die; a coherent strike can still kill before that. Root closure removes their sustainable access throughout the paired volumes. This limitation should be observed in Chapter 2's retreat and taught in Chapter 6, then relied on in Chapter 31. Killing Sus alone does not close a gradient.

Eda is both a clinician and a registered civil casualty-response convener. Her independent emergency powers are introduced through routine work in Chapter 6 and stated in Chapter 15, before they affect the climax. She can convene review, request marshals, preserve medical records, and protect service access under an issued order; she cannot convict, command all Wardens, or operate the root. Sus contests that order using field authority and loyal staff, producing a split command rather than instant universal compliance.
