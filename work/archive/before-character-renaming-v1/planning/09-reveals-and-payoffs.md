# REVEALS, PAYOFFS, AND EMOTIONAL RHYTHM

## Mystery contract

Suspense comes from consequences and uncertain choices, not from hiding thoughts the viewpoint character is actually having. Toby suspects an actual future self at once; he can consider that the vision shows root closure after Chapter 12. The reader may infer Sus's exploitation before Toby has adequate proof. This is dramatic tension, not a failed twist. Do not delay the inference by making Toby inexplicably incurious.

## Motifs and poetic opportunities

Use grain/measure for moral accounting, water/routes for consequences, names/records for personhood, hands/shared work for intimacy and limits. Let their meaning change. A motif should appear because a person is doing something, not because every scene needs symbolic decoration.

Reserve the strongest lyrical compression for six pivots: Chapter 1's coerced measure; Chapter 5's departure; Chapter 18's ordinary desired life; Chapter 22's small repair; Chapter 29's farewell; Chapter 32's planting. Chapter 31 earns spectacle and bodily detail more than a sermon.

Optional original tonal seeds, not mandatory dialogue or claims from the source manuscript:

- Toby after limited work: “The water reached the houses before anyone thought to thank him.”
- Maria after an honest count: “A name did not make a person safe. It made leaving them out a choice.”
- Farewell narration: “He could ask her to stay. He could not make staying the proof of love.”
- Final sowing: “They planted with hands that still remembered what was missing.”

Do not insert all four automatically. Their surrounding action must justify them, and a plainer line may be better. Avoid repeating the same idea in dialogue, narration, and a closing aphorism within one scene.
