## Chosen design and why

Thirty-two chapters, ninety-six scene cards; summed chapter budgets approximately **93,200 words**, with a flexible overall range of 88,000–100,000. Third-person close past tense. Toby principal POV; Maria in Chapters 4, 7, 10, 14, 20, 23, 26, 28, and 32. Five movements: occupied home, learning/evidence, failed intervention/temptation, loss/responsibility, collective rescue/permanent separation.

Aras is a fictional coastal village of about eighty households, native Telassar its occupying palace kingdom. Ancient Year 718 and future Year 3318 are paired 2,600 years apart. Veyr occupies the changed landscape. The Concord operates the lattice; Wardens are its field personnel. Schlelberg is runaway overlap of the paired inhabited volumes, not a late god or unexplained universal darkness.

Toby's practical water/repair skills matter before his combat training. He is acquired as a living reference through a broken regulator, not royal blood. Severance, called the Unbroken Edge ceremonially, is the last functional root-cutting key with real legendary history. Shield penetration takes close sustained coupling, dropping the attacker's own protection. Partner cover matters. Sus's bypass suppresses recovery time while exporting strain to people hidden in system classifications.

Maria investigates the labor system, then organizes captive survival and escape; her own flaw is false certainty and unilateral responsibility. Ipin's maintenance access supplies bounded evidence and logistics; he must prioritize people over perfect proof. Panji genuinely cares but concealed an old ninety-seven-person abandonment. Sus is initially competent and helpful, then exploits selective personhood accounting to preserve future infrastructure. Eda supplies independent medicine, civic review, and accountable emergency capacity.

One history, no rewrites or branching universes. A single forty-one-second sensory inception echo connects Day-0 acquisition to Day-84 root closure. It cannot carry bodies. Ordinary paired gates advance one day per day. One future root feeds all three ancient accesses—Storehouse, Salt, Quarry. Reference transfer needs seven idle days; captive tethering prevents that, and the first safe mass-release trough on Days 82–83 leaves only hours. Final root cut takes fifteen heartbeats of conscious contact in the future and consumes the reference and blade core. No spare portal afterward.

Toby's premature intervention saves twelve of sixty people but strands forty-eight, including Maria. Three die in confinement; two during rescue; forty-three survive, including Maria. Panji dies at Sus's hand in Chapter 21. The final rescue precedes Sus's defeat. Sus is detained alive by an established civic response. Toby gives up revenge to protect others and completes the cut, surviving with a persistent right-hand deficit. Maria could settle in Veyr but openly chooses the home and life she wants in her era. Their separation is an informed decision under established constraints, not an unexplained command to sacrifice love.

Future aftermath includes injury, displacement, rationing, and inquiry. Ancient aftermath wins limited garrison withdrawal and seasonal relief through a carrier coalition, not the fall of an empire. Maria's final planting scene shows a community continuing to live. The reader knows both lovers survive; neither receives a magic post-closure report about the other.

## Research and limits

Bronze Age material culture was grounded broadly in museum/archaeological references listed in the world bible: the Met's Mycenaean Civilization overview, Ashmolean Aegean World gallery, and Palace of Nestor material from the Ephorate of Antiquities of Messenia. These support inspiration, not claims that Aras's rituals or political institutions are historical Greek facts. Future physics is deliberately fictional and judged for internal consistency.

The plan was checked for structural completeness and major continuity dependencies. That is not proof of compelling prose, perfect future execution, historical exhaustiveness, or commercial success. The editorial audit names live craft risks: bureaucratic monotony, an overly engineered separation, insufficient mythic feeling, excessive grief, overperfect Maria, and unearned combat mastery. Those must be assessed in actual scenes.
