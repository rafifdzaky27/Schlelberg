# CHARACTER AND RELATIONSHIP BIBLE

All ages, motives, and backstories here are design decisions for the reconstruction, not facts recovered from the old draft. Keep five central characters. Supporting characters have bounded jobs; do not expand each into another secret lineage.

## Toby — 23 at Chapter 1

Aras farmer and sluice repairer; physically capable, practically observant, initially poor at literate administration and entirely untrained against organized fighters. Weather-darkened skin, working hands, dark hair long enough to tie; appearance is ordinary within his region. His mother's leaf mark on a jar is a family object, not a hereditary crest. His parents died of an ordinary fever three years before the conquest; introduce briefly only when context needs it. Do not add a murdered sibling or royal parent. Saman is an older neighbor, not a secret father.

Want: restore safety and agency to his home, then recover Maria and the others. Need: distinguish responsibility from total control and accept the judgments of people he loves. Wound: fleeing the fallen gate and later failing to protect seed have made strength feel equivalent to moral worth. False belief: with enough power he can prevent anyone important to him from having to lose. Strength: persistence, observation of flows and dependencies, willingness to work, ability to remember individuals rather than categories. Flaw: treats refusal or delay as betrayal when frightened; commits before checking how a system distributes costs.

Contradiction: he resents decisions made over his head, then makes decisions for Maria. Secret: initially hides the extent of his attraction to Sus's bypass and the residual warning signs. Boundary: he will not deliberately use an innocent person as expendable bait; his arc tests whether he notices when his 'necessary' action does effectively that.

Arc decisions: resist seed seizure (fails); accept a stated bargain with Panji (conditional trust); choose training while impatient (earned progress); cut the transfer early (saves twelve, strands forty-eight); accept bypass after a useful success (culpability); refuse captive sacrifice during the breach (costly reversal); almost relinquish role (low point); disclose the risk and accept others' revisions (leadership); honor Maria's decision; prioritize the route over killing Sus; complete severance knowing separation is permanent.

End: alive in Veyr, with a persistent right-hand tremor/weak grip following root overload. He can work and learn adaptations; do not render him useless or spiritually perfected. He has no automatic command throne. He assists recovery with Ipin and participates in public testimony.

Voice: concrete, initially terse when ashamed; farming/repair comparisons only where he would actually make them. Vocabulary expands through instruction without becoming engineering fluency overnight. His close narration can be lyrical, but he does not speak in continuous maxims. Anger often precedes embarrassment; humor emerges around ordinary work.

## Maria — 24

Aras resident, longtime companion and developing lover of Toby. Helps manage shared stores and mill work. Her competence comes from years of measuring grain, tracking household obligations, and coordinating people. She reads familiar quantities and names on administrative tallies but is not fluent in every palace document. Dark hair, burned shawl trimmed unevenly, damaged thumb skin; later fatigue and specific clothing repairs persist.

Want: keep her community alive and preserve its ability to choose how it lives. Need: let others share responsibility and tell the truth about uncertainty instead of promising more than she can deliver. Wound: as stores assistant she once obeyed an allocation that excluded an unlisted widow's household; quiet remembered failure, not a new tragedy subplot. False belief: if she stops holding everything together, everyone else will collapse. Strength: negotiation, memory for people and quantities, practical triage, ability to earn cooperation. Flaw: overwork, impatience with flight, manipulates hope when afraid a group will fragment.

Contradiction: resents Toby deciding for her but initially assigns tasks to other captives without asking. Secret: spends part of the protected seed reserve during hunger before telling the households whose future crop it represents. She later discloses the decision and accepts objections. Boundary: does not buy her own freedom by selecting an unwilling person to replace her.

Arc: negotiate two jars; organize supply and information while Toby is away; test rather than worship the future promise; stay to guide workers during the failed intervention; build captive cooperation; a failed escape and deaths expose the danger of false certainty; disclose what she does and does not know; delegate essential evacuation tasks; survive through combined actions; choose her own era openly, without declaring Toby selfish for needing to leave.

Romance: they know each other's working habits and share pre-existing affection, but neither starts with an unexamined marriage claim. Chapter 5 admits they want a future together. Chapter 13 reunion includes affection and a concrete disagreement. Chapter 18 communication revisits an ordinary shared memory and establishes that saving her alone is not her wish. Chapter 29 is a mutual, informed goodbye. No jealousy triangle. No sexual violence as shorthand for occupation or captivity. If intimacy is included, keep it brief, consensual, and emotional rather than explicit; no scene is required merely to establish adulthood.

End: living in Aras, co-organizing reconstruction rather than ruling alone. She grieves an inaccessible living person and still makes plans. Do not frame her as a widow waiting for a portal to reopen or imply she must remain romantically frozen forever.

Voice: direct, specific, capable of dry correction. Under strain she asks for counts, time, and names. She can be unfair when exhausted; other captives may successfully challenge her. Her POV includes bodies, storage, tasks, competing needs, and beauty unrelated to Toby.

## Ipin — 22

Veyr maintenance and route-record technician. Slim, quick with his hands, cropped hair, repaired work jacket, a practical tool roll; ordinary social standing. No hidden nobility, prodigy who understands everything, or comic accent. He knows his system because he services it.

Want: expose the discrepancy between transport records and living people, and keep service-ward residents from paying for decisions made above them. Need: make truth serve people rather than his desire to be vindicated. Wound: has spent eighteen months signing manifests he believed described inert loads; discovers that his work helped conceal human transfers. This is complicity, not an extra murdered-relative revenge story. False belief: once the complete evidence is secured, justice will follow automatically. Strength: precise observation, repair, permissions knowledge, willingness to question rank. Flaw: hoards information until proof is perfect and can prioritize a recorder over an endangered person.

Relationship with Toby: mutual irritation becomes earned partnership. He resents the resources allocated to one outsider while his ward struggles; Toby resents being treated as a calibration problem. Shared maintenance work lets each see the other's competence. No romantic competition over Maria.

Decisions: help Toby understand care instead of mocking him; preserve contradictory logs; confront Toby's selective concern; risk credentials on a verified rescue; abandon the most complete archive to get living people out in Chapter 21; rebuild a sufficient evidence chain with Eda; openly accept the danger of covering Toby's shield-down cuts; stay after victory as a friend, not a follower awarded to the hero.

Limits: cannot unlock every door, manufacture a new root, learn lost Maker physics overnight, or replace a reference without the stated calibration. His bypasses exploit access he already has or hardware shown earlier. He suffers a left-forearm burn in Chapter 21; fine work remains slower for the rest of the book.

Voice: exact, skeptical, often funny about concrete inconvenience. Jokes are not constant tension-breaking. In grief he becomes unusually spare. Avoid making him an internet-savvy contemporary dropped into fantasy speech.

## Panji — 58

Senior Warden instructor; field experience with ancient communities. Calm because he prepares, not because the narrative labels him wise. Gray hair, an old stiffness in the left knee, plain training clothes. Speaks the ancient regional language and can enter Aras under an existing technical-services arrangement with the occupation authorities.

Want: contain Schlelberg and prevent Toby repeating earlier failures. Need: surrender the protection afforded by secrecy and let others judge the information. Wound: twelve paired years earlier, he signed closure of a route during an incursion, abandoning ninety-seven people in the ancient zone. He believed it would prevent a larger loss, then permitted records to remove their personhood. False belief: telling novices everything before they are ready will cause another catastrophe. Strength: listening, teaching in increments, patience under pressure, tactical restraint. Flaw: paternalism and delayed disclosure.

Contradiction: teaches Toby to respect other people's choices while withholding the information necessary for choice. Secret: his signature, his knowledge of the accounting convention, and his failure to confront Sus earlier. Boundary: he will not knowingly feed current captives into a planned stabilization once an actual alternative is available; his previous failure makes this decision costly, not effortless.

Relationship with Sus: they once saved people together. Sus's competence gives Panji reason to rely on him; their disagreement is about who can impose costs and how long emergency authority lasts. Panji cannot simply arrest him without evidence and is compromised by the same records. Do not make that excuse limitless: he admits he also feared exposure.

Decisions: provide real care as part of recruitment; teach restraint; admit the earlier order when confronted rather than lie again; try to stop the live bypass; hold the isolation shutter; reject Sus's command to release it on civilians; die from Sus's direct attack while keeping the exit available. No magical cleansing blast, transferred soul, or strength download.

End: dead in Chapter 21; no resurrection. His record is published truthfully. Toby can love him without restoring a spotless legend.

Voice: practical questions, particular remembered mistakes, some warmth. He sometimes answers too late. A useful line must answer a present problem, not sound like a detached inspirational quotation.

## Sus — 46

Concord field commander and Toby's combat instructor. Fit, economical, observant. Initially saves people in view of Toby, remembers equipment limits, and improves a student's survival. No early sneering, villain costume, repeated 'dark eyes,' or narrator declarations that he is evil.

Want: preserve Veyr's stability and his authority to determine acceptable losses; retain the root as infrastructure and strategic power. Need he refuses: recognize ancient inhabitants as equal persons and submit his decisions to informed scrutiny. Wound: an earlier delayed evacuation in Veyr killed people while administrators argued; he learned the wrong general lesson that disagreement itself is the enemy. This explains but does not excuse him. False belief: a competent leader's calculation creates the right to compel compliance.

Strength: genuine tactical judgment, bravery, clear instruction, operational planning. Flaw: selective moral accounting, refusal to relinquish control, suppression of inconvenient evidence. Contradiction: keeps promises to individuals while designing systems that exploit classes of people. Secret: backing the prospecting arrangement, using detainees as stabilizers, and suppressing feasible disconnection planning.

Why he needs Toby: direct reference access cannot be coerced into precise sustained cuts merely by moving Toby's hand. Toby must consciously maintain coupling. Sus therefore recruits trust and urgency. He offers a plausible selective rescue before demanding wider consent. If Toby refuses, the emergency purge remains his terminal alternative; neither brainwashing nor infinite mind control exists.

Why he tolerates Panji: Panji's authority and teaching are needed for legitimate recruitment and initial calibration. Sus expects shared culpability to keep him quiet. He kills Panji only when the latter is physically preventing the seizure of control, making the act instrumental rather than a random reveal.

Escalation: manipulate classifications; offer useful shortcuts; seek Toby's assent; use live operation to consolidate control; kill Panji; blame Toby; attempt one-sided purge when the rescue removes captive leverage. End: disarmed alive, detained by civil emergency personnel supported by Eda's evidence. No 'I will return' stinger. His policy faces public examination; a full courtroom sequel is not required.

Voice: controlled and specific, often persuasive. He calls costs by precise quantities until confronted with names. Allow him one true criticism of Toby's recklessness that Toby cannot dismiss.

## Supporting cast and limits

**Eda, 41:** independent future clinician. Introduced Chapter 6 treating Toby; treats a Warden and an unregistered person by need rather than rank. Can arrange care, evaluate load injuries, invoke civil casualty protocols, and call accountable emergency guards. Cannot operate the root herself. Demands a rescue plan with actual capacity and evacuation arrangements before helping fugitives. No romance subplot.

**Saman, 61:** Aras neighbor and practical worker. Chapter 2 left-leg crush injury; treatment and a brace, no effortless recovery. Knows old drains and local work crews; helps logistics in Chapter 27, not an unexpected front-line fighter. Survives. His trust is not automatic after Toby's intervention goes wrong.

**Lio, 11:** the water boy already seen in Chapter 1, named Chapter 2. Enslaved by the occupation, freed into Maria's care during the first breach, later among the forty-eight captives. Has specific small wants—his own name used, a dry place to sleep, keeping a repaired cup—not oracle wisdom. Survives; performs a bounded signaling task, not adult combat.

**Daran, 39:** the unnamed underground overseer in Chapter 1. Native Telassari surveyor/prospector working through Sus's intermediaries. Has plans of the receiver and understands practical instructions, not chronology. Thinks recovering the relic system will earn status. Supervises rations to keep captive anchors alive. Dies in Chapter 28 when his attempt to force a path through a baffle exposes him to an Iwang. Maria does not engineer his death or celebrate it.

**Captain Theren, 44:** Chapter 1 levy officer, distinct from Daran. Wants a workable occupied district and his quota, not apocalypse. Initially brutal. Later agrees to a temporary withdrawal/relief charter when his position is untenable, neighboring villages control supply, and Daran's scheme has endangered his men. Pragmatic concession, not conversion into a benevolent hero. Keep him peripheral.

**Neris, 67:** Aras midwife and storehouse contributor; introduced Chapter 4, among forty-eight. Challenges Maria's overwork and delegates care. Survives.

**Oran, 27:** stoneworker, among forty-eight; introduced Chapter 14. Initially favors leaving weakened prisoners behind, later takes an agreed dangerous task rather than being shamed into sacrifice. Dies during Chapter 28 exit failure while attempting to hold a physical support. His change must be shown in two preceding scenes.

Prisoner deaths: three during confinement, whose names Maria records (Thesa, Pell, Anet); introduce at least one in a living action before death. During evacuation Oran and Hessa die despite attempts to help. Hessa is identified in Chapter 23 as medically fragile and refuses being silently written off. Forty-three of the original forty-eight survive; Daran is a separate captor, not part of that count.

## Relationship map: pressures, not misunderstandings

**Bounded action support:** Jora, 36, and Senn, 30, are trained Warden shield operators, first seen assisting Chapter-8 exercises. Both accept the corroborated civil rescue plan in Chapters 24–25. They are not additional mentors or major POVs. They cover the Day-82/83 ancient rescue, return through Salt ahead of Toby in Chapter 29, help stop the purge and cover the final cut, and survive. Jora is cautious about civilian loads; Senn asks practical route questions. Give them recognizable behavior without adding secret histories. Ipin coordinates shields and timing; these trained operators supply the sustained combat cover a technician cannot plausibly provide alone.

Toby–Maria: protection versus self-determination; incomplete communication has physical causes, but the decisive disagreement remains after they can speak.

Toby–Ipin: personal urgency versus equal accounting; shared practical work turns friction into trust. Each must accept correction from the other.

Toby–Panji: real affection versus paternal concealment; truth fractures authority without erasing care.

Toby–Sus: needed competence versus seductive certainty; the betrayal hurts because help was real.

Panji–Sus: shared history and culpability split by willingness to disclose and stop. Neither is Toby's literal father.

Maria–captives: organization versus consent; she learns to distribute responsibility. People disagree for real reasons, not because they fail to explain an obvious plan.

Ipin–Eda: proof versus treatment; she requires sufficient evidence and actionable capacity, he learns that perfect records can be less urgent than living patients.
