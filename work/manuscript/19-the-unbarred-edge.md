# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Nineteen — The Unbarred Edge

The first thing the higher gain gave Tobious was speed. The second was pain he could not describe without sounding as if he had swallowed a live wire.

On the first day he mistook the speed for control. He completed one guarded cut and lifted Severance before Ypin called the measured reset. The blade accepted a second contact sooner than it should have. The artificial sink took the draw, but the handle jerked in his grip. His next step carried him across the painted cover line. Jorra pulled him back by the collar before the practice barrier discharged. No one had been hurt; the trial had worked as designed in the narrow sense and failed in the wider one.

“You were watching the blade,” she said.

“It was ready.”

“I wasn't.”

He had used the shorter interval to outrun the person assigned to keep him alive. In the next trial, Jorra called her position before Ypin permitted contact. Suss did not object. He wanted the setting to be usable, not merely impressive on a display.

Edda made him stop after every measured trial. She checked his pulse, his hand, the small changes in balance he tried to hide by standing still. Suss kept the gain low at first, increasing it only when the monitoring display showed that the artificial sink could take another load. Panjios stood beyond the practice barrier and watched every setting. Ypin ran the service trace. No one called the method forbidden in the room; they called it an emergency setting, which was both more accurate and easier to consent to.

With Severance's ordinary guarded cut, Tobious waited thirty seconds before the core could settle. With the gain raised, the second cut became available while he could still feel the first in his arm. That change was real. It also left his fingers trembling when he tried to lift a cup afterward.

The cup mattered more to Edda than his claim that he could still hold the blade. She asked him to trace a line on a slate with the same fingers. It wandered twice. He could force a grip around a large handle; he could not make the small corrections that told a healer his hand had recovered. She wrote the difference in the record while he watched.

“Stop for today,” Edda said on the third session.

“The hand is steady enough.”

“You have spilled water twice while telling me that.”

He looked at the damp rim of the table. She had not exaggerated.

Panjios asked Suss whether the root's temporary draw had been kept entirely on the artificial sink. Suss showed the rated capacity. Panjios asked for the live route behind the rating. Suss said the trial had not required that permission level. Tobious listened from the medical chair.

Suss did not dismiss the question. He offered a view of the sealed sink and the heat it had taken, enough to show that the local test load had not failed. Panjios insisted that the return path beyond the sink could still matter during a live cut. Tobious could hear the two men arguing from measurements neither possessed in full. Earlier he would have trusted Panjios to be the cautious one and Suss to be the capable one. Since the archive, he found it harder to let either role settle the argument for him.

“You didn't tell me about the ninety-seven until I found your signature,” he said to Panjios. “If the trace is missing, tell me what measurement would prove you right.”

Panjios's face closed, then opened with an effort. “A full routing trace of the return load. I have asked for it.”

“And when they give it?”

“We will examine it together.”

Tobious wanted to believe the promise. It had arrived after the years when it would have been easiest to trust.

***

On Day Fifty-three, a trial at a cooling branch became a rescue before Suss could shut it down.

Ypin and a crew member had entered a service bay to verify that the practice load remained isolated. The cooling shutter dropped behind them when its guide failed. Tobious heard the impact through the floor. A warning light on Ypin's board changed from green to a color he had learned to fear. The temperature inside the bay began climbing; if the return water stopped altogether, the pressure would push into nearby homes.

A service dispatcher appeared at the outer pane with a list of those homes. One housed a night school whose children were still inside; another had patients waiting for a cooling cabinet to be repaired. The dispatcher was already calling the ward to move them. She did not ask Tobious to save the whole district. She asked how many minutes she could tell the residents they had. Suss gave her a number with a margin wide enough for retreat. Tobious listened to the dispatcher repeat it without rounding down. The people beyond the wall were not an abstract consequence of a failed return line.

Suss mapped the two shutter points. The first cut would open the worker side, the second would free the service return before its heat rose too far. Under ordinary recovery, Severance would wait between them. The trapped crew did not have thirty seconds for the second while the guide was still bending.

“High gain,” Suss said. “Two cuts. No pursuit beyond the bay.”

Jorra put her shield between Tobious and the failing guide. Sennos held a line at the door. Tobious set the blade to the first seam. He made the three-heartbeat contact cleanly, feeling his own protection drop. The shutter opened enough for Ypin to push the crew member toward the gap. A metal fragment struck Jorra's field and drove her shoulder against the wall. She held.

“Return's next,” Ypin called. “Don't let it back into the housing.”

The core had not settled. Suss raised the gain. Tobious felt heat gather in the grip, then set the edge to the second seam. He counted three beats, though the last seemed to stretch. The channel opened. The return water found its proper course with a sound like a river forced into a narrow bed.

The strain ran through his elbow after he lifted the blade. For a moment he could not tell whether his shield had returned. He waited for Jorra to call cover before stepping back. She did, sharply, as though she knew he might move without her. The saved minutes were real; so was how close the technique brought him to trusting his own sensation more than the team's signal.

Ypin pulled the crew member through. She was conscious but burned at one forearm. Tobious caught Ypin's sleeve when he stumbled, and the four of them retreated behind Jorra's line. The bay cooled. In the rooms beyond the wall, people who had been close to losing water would only notice a brief interruption.

Suss checked the injured worker before looking at the instrument. “Both out?”

“Both,” Sennos said.

“Then the method did its job.”

Tobious wanted to agree. Ypin was breathing beside him. The worker was on a stretcher, in pain and alive. The usefulness of the faster cut had never been more obvious.

The dispatcher returned to say the school and clinic had moved people clear. The warning in those homes would be lifted after the line held through another cycle. They had not needed to see Severance to benefit from the cut. Tobious liked that more than he wanted to admit. He had wanted a power that could protect people he did not know. Now it had, and the evidence of that good made the thin spike on his monitor harder to face.

On Tobious's private monitor, a narrow spike appeared beneath the artificial-load trace. It had the Storehouse branch's signature. He asked Suss about it while Edda sealed the worker's burn.

“Settling from the shared root,” Suss said. “The return jumped when the shutter moved. It should be temporary.”

“Was the ancient branch carrying it?”

“The local sink took the trial draw. I will have command confirm the route.”

Tobious looked for Ypin. He was helping the stretcher team make the turn. By the time he returned, the spike had faded from the live screen. The private monitor had stored it.

“I need the full routing trace,” Ypin said after Tobious described the line. Tobious kept the saved file closed in his hand.

“Suss asked command to confirm it.”

“I need it before another high-gain cut.”

Tobious did not disagree. He also knew what would happen if he filed the anomaly as a possible live-load injury without proof: the practice would stop, the Day-57 operation might be cancelled, and Mariya would spend more time in the annex while the Day-84 window drew closer.

Ypin held out his hand for the device. Tobious could have given it to him. A dated file and Ypin's independent reading would have made it harder for Suss to call the spike settling noise. Instead Tobious turned the screen dark and said he needed to compare the calibration on the next trial. Ypin let his hand fall. Their friendship did not give him authority to seize it; it had made him expect Tobious to offer it.

“Let me compare it with the next measured trial,” he said.

Ypin's mouth tightened. “That's an answer for later.”

“I know.”

He did not show Ypin the stored spike. He told himself a second reading would make the first useful. The choice gave him one more day of the method he wanted to work.

***

On Day Fifty-five, Tobious signed the Day-57 operation plan. Its stated goal was a live survey and staged release preparation for all forty-seven captives. The first step would cut a rated support line under supervision. Suss's command team held the full routing map. Panjios had attached an objection to the high-gain setting. Edda's medical restrictions were listed beside Tobious's name.

Panjios came to the signing room with his objection on a separate page. He did not ask Tobious to trust him because he had warned him about ninety-seven people. “My signature on the old order is a reason to demand better proof from me,” he said. “It is also a reason I will not call a return route safe because its first column looks clean.”

“You asked for the trace,” Tobious said.

“Yes. If it comes before the operation, read it with Ypin. If it does not, make the delay part of your decision.”

Tobious resented the phrasing. He had wanted Panjios to tell him whether to sign, then hated himself for wanting to put the choice back in the older man's hands. The page stayed beside the plan. Panjios left it there without touching Tobious's pen.

Ypin asked again for the trace. Command provided a summary showing temporary network draw below its stated limit. It did not show who or what received the draw. Ypin filed a request for the underlying route. The response time was longer than the time left before the operation.

Edda examined Tobious before signing the medical line. He could sustain one standard guarded cut with support. The high-gain setting still left tremor after repetition. She approved his presence at the survey, not unlimited use of the setting. Tobious asked whether the plan would be canceled if he refused high gain. She said she could not know what Suss would do with a refusal. She wrote that answer on the margin, where it could not later become a doctor's consent to the whole route.

Tobious placed his private device beneath the paper while she wrote. He could feel its thin edge against his wrist. The saved spike was a dated reason to stop and ask who bore the extra draw. He did not put it on the table. He knew by then that he was not waiting for better evidence; he was protecting a method that might bring Mariya out before the window closed.

Tobious could have told Edda about the Storehouse spike. He could have demanded the underlying trace before signing. He pictured Mariya in the annex, the clicking wall, the counted water jars. He also remembered the two people pulled from the cooling shutter while the core was still warm. The technique had saved them.

He signed.

That night his right hand trembled when he untied his wrist support. He held it under cool water and watched the movement subside. On the private device by his bed, the old spike remained. It had no name beside it. He let the display go dark without sending it to anyone.
