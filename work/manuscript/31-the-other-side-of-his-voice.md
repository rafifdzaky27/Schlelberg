# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Thirty-One — The Other Side of His Voice

Tobious recognized the skyway before it failed.

From the service lip beneath the High Concourse, he could see a pale strip of sky between buildings taller than the hills of Aras. Roads joined their upper reaches without pillars beneath them. A long silver carriage passed along one. He had seen it all through ankle-deep water on the ninth day of the occupation, before he understood what a city could build above empty air.

The root seam lay below his feet, behind a narrow guard rail. Ypin checked the line from the service station. Jorra and Sennos held the approach with their shields, already warm from the purge junction. Edda had cleared the nearest concourse and warned people away from the skyway. Most had left. A few were still crossing when the overlap reached the support beneath it.

The road bowed downward.

Tobious saw figures falling from it. In the moment, they were people too far away for his hand to catch. He gripped his wounded right ribs, felt blood pass between his fingers, and stepped onto the maintenance lip.

An opening appeared in the air across the root's light. It was not a gate. The breeze on Tobious's face came from Veyr's failing concourse; beyond the opening, the water around another man's ankles remained still. An oil lamp burned behind him. Darran held his shoulder. The soldier stood on the stairs. A younger Tobious looked out from beneath the ancestor terrace, his right wrist swollen, his hair tied back, his eyes fixed on the impossible city.

Tobious looked first at Darran. The overseer was standing near the broken frame with the drawing that had begun the excavation. Then Tobious looked at himself.

He knew the forty-one seconds had already been lived from the other side. He could not send a new history through them. He could speak the words he had once heard, and they were still worth saying to the frightened man before him.

“Don't go with him.”

The younger man's face changed. He knew the voice before he knew the scar. Tobious reached toward him. The skyway gave way behind Tobious, the fall drawing his attention for a fraction of a breath. The small shapes dropped between the towers.

“Tobi. Listen. They aren't looking for stores.”

A crash came from the passage on the other side. Lios's jug broke on the ancient stone. The soldier turned to shout at the boy. Tobious stepped close enough for the light to show the scar beneath his left eye. He felt how short his hair had become against his collar and how much blood was gathering at his right side.

“That door is what they came for. And now they know you can open it.”

The opening went black when the forty-first second ended. He had no chance to add a warning about Suss, Panjios, or the forty-eight. There had never been one. The young man would still have to decide with the knowledge available to him, as Tobious had.

The root's pressure rose. Jorra called for him to come back from the edge. Tobious turned toward the seam. The people beneath Aras were out; Mariya had chosen to remain there. Suss's purge had been disarmed. The last task could now be done without making either group the hidden support for the other.

Across the concourse, emergency crews reached the skyway's near end. One lowered a line toward a broken ledge where a passenger still moved. Another waved those on the intact section back from its edge. Tobious took a step toward them. Jorra caught his arm.

The passenger on the ledge wore a bright work sash. She had caught a rail with one hand and was trying to pull a smaller person close enough to reach the line. A rescuer crawled out under a tether whose anchor Tobious could see fixed to the solid span. The scene was not a blur of falling shapes now. These were people making choices in the remaining seconds, and the crews had a route toward them. Tobious wanted to run there because the danger was visible. The root's rising note came through his boots from a danger that would soon reach streets he could not see.

“They have the road,” she said. “We have the seam.”

He could see the crew working; they were not waiting for a sword. The root load rose another mark. If he left the seam until everyone on the skyway had been found, a larger part of the city might follow the first span down. He had once mistaken going toward the nearest visible danger for taking responsibility for everyone. Now he allowed people with lines and training to do their work while he did his.

Ypin held up the final reading. The maintenance lip would remain accessible for one more load interval. Sennos checked the reserve charge in his shield. Jorra set her feet by the rail and told Tobious where to lean if his wounded ribs gave way. They spoke as workers checking a dangerous task, each admitting the point where their part could fail.

***

Severance met the root seam without a flash.

The blade's dark channel filled from hilt to tip. Tobious set his feet on the maintenance lip and gave the core the living feedback it needed. The contact would have to last fifteen heartbeats without interruption. If he let go early, the reference would rebound through the damaged root. Ypin had shown him the measurement. His own body now had to make it true.

On the first beat, the seam pressed back against the blade. On the second, his shield disappeared. Jorra raised cover between him and a line of stone fragments falling from the concourse. The third beat traveled up his injured right arm like a blow delivered through bone.

At four, the root drew the three ancient access lines bright on Ypin's board. Storehouse, Salt, Quarry: three doors fed by one material. At five, Sennos held an Iwang away from the service lip. It had come toward the active gradient, its hollow torso sounding under the rush of air. He did not need to kill it. He needed to keep it from Tobious while the current remained.

Six. Tobious felt the blade's grip heat beneath his fingers. Seven. A part of the city went dark beyond the concourse, and the backup lights in Edda's evacuated ward came on. The preparations had not prevented damage. They had put people somewhere it could be survived.

Eight. The load shifted from the root into the physical seam. His right forearm seized. He could still feel his hand on the grip but could no longer close it harder. Nine. Ypin called the reading: the reference mineral was breaking, as the model had predicted. Ten. The skyway's far end stopped moving. People on the safe side ran back toward solid floor.

At eleven, Tobious nearly let go. Pain had made the cut smaller than the words *save two eras*. It was a hand failing on a tool. Jorra said she could take his weight; she did not claim she could finish the contact. Tobious leaned against the rail she held for him and kept his left hand on the blade.

Twelve. The Storehouse line vanished from Ypin's board. Thirteen. Salt followed. Fourteen. The Quarry's damaged signal went silent.

On the fifteenth beat, Severance's opaque core cracked without a sound he could hear. Light ran through the root seam and ended there. The blade became ordinary metal in his hand, its active channel dark. The mineral reference had been consumed. No temporal access remained on either shore.

On Ypin's board, the two inhabited volumes ceased pulling at one another. The Schlelberg trace fell out of the danger band and then went blank with the root. Tobious could not see Mariya's shore. He could see that the line by which the ages had been made to bear one another was gone.

The Iwang at the service lip lost its clear shape. It thinned along a boundary no longer fed by a gradient and withdrew into the failing gap. Another collapsed beneath Sennos's field. Ordinary shield units still shone around the operators; the root had never been the source of every light in Veyr. Beyond them, some city services faltered, and others continued on backup power.

A public route display above them went dark. The emergency lamps took several breaths to come on. During those breaths Tobious heard the unmasked city: people calling from the concourse, a stretcher wheel striking a joint in the floor, water still moving in a pipe that had been given independent power. Then a clinic signal answered from the lower ward. It told Ypin that the first backup reservoir had held.

On the far side of the maintenance lip, the city did not become still when the root ended. A tram slowed under its own reserve power and stopped before a gap. People climbed out through a service hatch and followed a worker with a lamp down the safe stairs. Somewhere above, a food tower lost its bright growing lamps; smaller emergency strips came on along the handrails. The cut had changed their world irreversibly. It had not turned every skill or tool they had built into dust.

Tobious's grip opened. He fell toward the maintenance floor, not over the lip. Ypin and Jorra reached him before the wounded ribs struck the stone. Ypin's burned forearm could not bear his whole weight, so Sennos took the other side. Together they carried him through the service approach while Edda's team came up with a stretcher.

Tobious tried to ask whether Mariya had seen the Salt Gate close. There was no path by which an answer could return. The last thing he saw before the clinic lights replaced the concourse was Severance in Ypin's hand, its broken core no longer shining.

***

Three weeks later, Veyr had bridges made for temporary use and queues for water that stretched past gardens no longer cooled by the root. Some families were still living in borrowed rooms. Food towers had lost a harvest; their workers replanted under independent power while the city argued over whose ration would change first. The skyway's three dead had names on the inquiry's first public page. The city did not hide them beneath a category called structural load.

Tobious stood in one of the queues once, against Edda's advice to avoid the crowd until his ribs healed. The woman who had sold greens from her window was ahead of him. She had lost plants in the move and was trading their surviving seeds for a place in another growing room. She recognized him, took her measured water, and said, “I still want my old room back.”

“I know.”

“The clinic kept my neighbor's medicine cold. Tell them that too when they count what worked.”

He promised to tell the inquiry both things. She did not ask him to thank her for allowing him to stand in line. They waited while a reservoir worker adjusted the flow, and each person took the amount the ward could supply that morning.

Tobious had watched Edda read those names to the families before the public page appeared. The crews had saved several people from the broken span. They had not reached three. One family asked why the city had kept anyone crossing so close to the measured deadline. The transit supervisor answered with the evacuation order and the delayed carriage records. The explanation did not bring their son home. It did show which decision could be examined and changed, rather than leaving them with a disaster called inevitable.

The woman with the bright work sash had survived. She sat at the hearing with one arm held close to her body and told the supervisor that the last carriage had waited while a service ramp was checked. She did not know whether the delay was necessary. The ramp crew had a log. Tobious saw the supervisor request it instead of asking her to guess. She glanced at Tobious once, recognizing the man who had stood below with the sword, and then returned to the people examining her route. He could bear being looked at. He did not have an answer that belonged in place of the ramp log.

Suss remained in custody while civil investigators gathered testimony. His field decisions were examined alongside the records that had once protected them. Panjios's old closure of the ninety-seven was published with his final act at the isolation door. Tobious objected when a draft memorial called him redeemed. The words they chose instead described what he had done on both days. He could love the man who had taught him without asking the dead to pay for a spotless story.

Tobious testified too. He told the inquiry he had seen the far-floor warning before freeing the first twelve and that he had concealed the Storehouse spike after the cooling-shutter rescue. Suss's counsel asked whether his own intervention had not made everything worse. Tobious answered that it had harmed people and that the command's hidden routing had made the harm useful to its policy. Both statements could be true without one excusing the other. The clerk entered them under his name, where anyone reviewing the case could challenge them.

Edda examined Tobious's hand in the rehabilitation ward. The cut had left a persistent tremor in his right fingers and a grip weaker than it had been on the farm. Some days it tired quickly. She showed him how to brace a tool against his forearm and use the left hand for the fine adjustment. The scar beneath his left eye remained. The ribs were healing more slowly than he liked.

On the first day she asked him to pick up a cup with the right hand, he spilled half its water. He was angry at how small the task was. Edda gave him a cloth and let him dry the table. On the next attempt he used both hands. He drank without spilling, but the grip shook afterward. They recorded what he had done, not whether it looked graceful. At night he sometimes woke with the sensation of Severance's core breaking in his palm. By morning there was only his hand and another set of exercises to learn.

Ypin visited with a small water regulator from the lower ward. It needed a seating ring replaced, a job no one would put in a memorial. He set it on the table between them. Tobious held the housing with his left hand while Ypin used his recovering forearm to guide the ring. They stopped when either hand began to shake and began again after resting. The regulator held pressure on the third attempt.

When it worked, Tobious asked whether the lower ward had enough spare parts to keep replacing the rings. Ypin said the supply shop had begun cutting them from a new material, and that half of the first batch would probably fail the pressure test. They would have to tell the shop which half. The future beyond the root was not a single crisis ending in a single rescue. It was a long sequence of ordinary corrections, some of which his injured hand could still help make.

“You can come with me tomorrow,” Ypin said. “There are more than I can do alone.”

“I don't know all your valves.”

“You know when water is going somewhere it shouldn't. We'll work out the rest.”

Tobious looked past him at the city. Its tall roads still crossed the air, though one span ended in a clean temporary brace. He had survived the moment he had once seen from a cold chamber beneath Aras. He did not know how Mariya's next morning had begun. She did not know his hand could still mend a small thing. The separation would not become less real if he filled it with imagined messages.

He picked up the regulator. Its weight was manageable in his left hand. When the tremor came through the right, he waited for it to pass and kept working.
