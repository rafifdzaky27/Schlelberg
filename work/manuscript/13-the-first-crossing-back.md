# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Thirteen — The First Crossing Back

Mariya did not run when she saw Tobious at the end of the salt path.

She was waiting where the road rose through the olive trees, a bundle of work cloths under one arm. He had imagined the moment often enough to be embarrassed by what he had wanted from it: her hands around his neck, a day in which neither of them had to count anything. She walked toward him at her ordinary pace. Only when she was close did he see that her thumb had healed in a pale, uneven line and that the burned edge of her shawl had been mended with a piece of blue cloth.

“Your hair,” she said.

“It got in the way.”

“That answer applies to most of you.”

He laughed, and she put down the bundle. Their embrace made his right wrist ache; he did not move away. She felt familiar beneath a change of cloth and weeks of work. She rested her cheek against his shoulder for a long moment, then stepped back to look at him.

“What do you know?”

He had missed the precision of her questions. He took out the copy of the manifest. “Fifty-eight unregistered biological loads. Zero protected people. Pehl is there. I think others on your list are too. Suss authorized us to inspect and map the branch.”

“Can you get them out?”

“I don't know yet.”

Her eyes moved across his face. She could tell when he was trying to make uncertainty sound temporary. He let it remain what it was.

“Good,” she said. “That's an answer I can use.”

She told him who had been reassigned since her last wrapper and how Darran had begun moving carts through the red-marked door after dusk. She named the households that had lost a worker, the ones that still received a ration, and the ones that had stopped asking because the guards punished questions with extra shifts. Tobious wanted to ask about all of Aras at once. She made him listen to one route at a time.

Samon waited in his sister's yard. He could move from the door to a chair with a stick and someone close enough to catch him. The left leg was thinner beneath the brace. When Tobious thanked him for being alive, Samon waved the sentiment away and asked whether the new metal in Tobious's case could repair a water gate.

“It can cut a field,” Tobious said. “It may not help with a gate.”

“Then don't let anyone put it near my sluice.”

Mariya had made an arrangement for the sluice with the mason's brother, who had since discovered that he preferred walls. Samon had been explaining the water to him from a chair. Aras had continued without Tobious. The fact hurt for an instant, then relieved him. He had wanted home to be safe because of him; he was glad it had found ways to remain itself in his absence.

Near the well, Lios showed Tobious his cup. Its bottom came from the jug broken on the day of the opening, and its new rim had been smoothed by his aunt's hand. The boy examined Severance's case but did not ask to touch it.

“They still take water below,” he said. “I can carry it when there's no rope.”

“You don't have to.”

“I know.”

Mariya caught Tobious's glance. “He carries from the well to the outer hatch. The guard who allowed it is on duty today. I'll be with him.”

Tobious nearly told her to leave Lios away from the site. He had been gone six weeks. She had spent those weeks arranging safe paths and knowing which guard kept his word. He asked what the boy's aunt thought. Mariya said she had agreed to this one delivery because Lios's cup marked whose water reached the workers. The answer did not make the risk disappear. It made it an informed risk he did not own.

Panjios and Ypin joined them by the old storehouse. Jorra remained at the outer corridor to protect an exit; Sennos had the Salt relay and a route back to Veyr if the site failed. The team was small enough that Darran could call it an inspection without looking foolish.

Ypin took a thin wafer from a padded pocket and showed it to Mariya before asking her to carry it. It was no larger than her palm, with a shallow edge she could find in the dark.

“Touch here to record,” he said. “It will hold short messages until a diagnostic window sends them. You can see a mark when the packet leaves the wafer. That does not mean Tobious has read it.”

“How long does it last?”

“Several days if you use it only for brief recordings. Less if you leave the display bright. It doesn't open anything.”

“Can it tell you where I am?”

“Only when it reaches a fixed relay, and then it tells me which relay. It is an inspection recorder, not a guide to find you.”

Mariya turned it in her hand. “Then I'll tell you where I am.”

Ypin nodded. Tobious saw he had chosen to explain its limits first. A gift became more useful when its receiver knew what it could not do.

As the others prepared, Mariya pulled Tobious into the narrow shade behind the granary. The old steps were still there, smelling of flour and fig leaves. She kissed him. He had wondered whether six weeks and a thousand unshared moments would make the gesture uncertain. It did not. Her hand found the back of his neck; his left hand found the repaired blue edge of her shawl. When they parted, he could feel how much of the life he had imagined was still possible, if the world gave them time to choose it.

“Don't decide what I can bear because you have a blade now,” she said.

“I won't.”

He meant it when he said it. The promise would be tested before the day was over.

***

The Receiver no longer looked like a place people had uncovered by accident. Darran's crew had cleared the mud, shored the corridor, and fitted pale baffles along the walls. The black threshold under the lower steps remained shut, but the annex beyond it pulsed with a light so slight Tobious first noticed it in the water, not in the stone.

Mariya entered ahead of the team with a cart of food. Lios came with water for the outer hatch. Darran allowed them because the workers had complained of short rations and because a child carrying a cup did not look like part of a Warden inspection. Tobious disliked the calculation. He did not stop them. Ypin had already explained that counting the people where they actually stood required someone who could name them.

At the ninth step, Mariya touched his elbow. The water in the cracked bowl began to tremble. One high click, two low. The seam ahead opened. She waited through the guard's practiced count before crossing.

“The first time I tried this, I nearly lost a foot,” she said.

“You wrote that.”

“I wanted to know if you'd listened.”

The statement carried affection and anger together. Tobious had listened; he had not been here to pull her back. He followed her across.

In the annex, Darran's marked load area filled two connected chambers. A central support divided them: the near chamber led to the safe stair, the far one to a recessed work floor with no ordinary exit. Thin bands of light crossed between the support and the floor at ankle height. Tobious had seen fields in Veyr. He had never seen them laid through a room full of people as if people were part of the fastening.

Sixty were inside the marked area by Ypin's count. Mariya had come into the far chamber to hand out bread and compare faces to her board; Lios had set his water at the outer hatch and followed her when a guard said the far workers could not leave their posts to fetch it. The two new load marks had appeared when they crossed the boundary. Tobious saw them both beside Nerissa and Pehl. In the near chamber, twelve people stood behind a movable rail by the stair. The other forty-eight occupied the far floor and its surrounding work recesses.

“Sixty,” Ypin said into the team channel. “Includes the two delivery carriers. Twelve near, forty-eight far.”

“Are the carriers entered as load?” Suss's voice came back from Veyr, thin with static.

Ypin checked the local readout. “Since they crossed the boundary, yes.”

Tobious watched Mariya hand bread to Annet. She took a piece for herself only after Nerissa pushed one into her hand. The men at the central support were adjusting a stone weight. The fields brightened when a worker leaned against a marked wall, then dimmed when she moved. He understood enough to be afraid of what Ypin would say next.

Ypin opened the branch map on his portable board. Two routes left the central support. One carried the near chamber's load to the safe stair. The other passed beneath the far floor and returned by an unseen line toward the black frame. If the near route closed suddenly, the root would seek an equalizing path. The map drew that path through the forty-eight people standing on the far side.

“We need the physical supports mapped before any release,” Panjios said. “Darran, stop the weight adjustment.”

Darran held up a hand to his men. One obeyed; another kept turning a bronze handle in the wall. The click pattern changed. A field line crawled across the floor near the workers' feet.

“That is part of the inspection,” Darran said. “They cannot all stand idle.”

“They can for a few breaths,” Panjios answered.

Pehl stepped back from the line. A guard shoved him toward his station. When Pehl resisted, the guard raised the shaft of his spear. Annet moved between them. The spear point dropped toward her shoulder. At the same time, a pale strut pressed from the wall above the far chamber. Mineral clicking answered the altered baffle rhythm.

“There's an Iwang in the seam,” Jorra warned from the outer passage.

Tobious reached the central support. The near route was close enough for Severance to couple. He could cut it and free the twelve by the stair. He saw the far branch on Ypin's board in the same glance. Forty-eight people would carry the redistributed load until the root settled. How long that would take, he did not know.

“Wait,” Panjios said. “We can hold the guard. We need the second support.”

The guard struck Annet with the shaft. She fell beside the brightening seam. An Iwang limb came through the wall above her, gathering weight as it reached the chamber air. The twelve near the stair pushed against their rail. One called for a child on the far side. Darran shouted for his men to drive the workers back to their marks.

Tobious heard all the instructions and knew none would be obeyed quickly enough to stop the next blow. He remembered the granary jar slipping from his hands while seven soldiers stood around him. He had a blade now. The near route was in reach.

“Tobious,” Ypin said, seeing where he had placed the edge. “The load goes far.”

“I see it.”

He cut.

Three heartbeats. Jorra's shield rose between him and the guard. Severance passed through the marked line. The near chamber's fields collapsed, and the twelve by the stair stumbled free. Tobious shouted for them to move. Sennos at Salt relayed the opening; village helpers waiting above took the first two from the stair. The local guard's spear struck Jorra's field and skidded aside.

For an instant Tobious believed he could do it again.

Then the far chamber moved.

Light ran beneath its floor like water reaching a lower field. The central support contracted. Bands rose around the forty-eight, not ropes, but a pressure that made every attempt to step away pull at someone else. Pehl cried out as his ankles were drawn back to a painted mark. Mariya caught Annet and kept her from falling into the seam. Lios stood with his small cup in both hands, too frightened to put it down.

The Iwang pressed against the newly fed boundary. Its open torso filled with the light beyond it. Another answered from the recess. The twelve on the safe stair were out of its reach; the people Tobious had left on the far floor were not.

“Second cut,” Tobious said. “I can take the far line.”

Panjios seized his forearm before Severance reached it. “The far floor is carrying the frame. Cut that now and it falls on them.”

“Then hold the frame.”

“With what? We haven't mapped the supports.”

Ypin's board showed a load rising through the forty-eight bodies. Tobious could understand the warning now because he had made it true. Suss came over the channel, his voice hard and distinct despite the static.

“Withdraw the reference. Seal the near stair. The freed group goes to local care.”

“Mariya is in there,” Tobious said.

“I know she is there. A second blind cut kills the group.”

Tobious looked across the floor. Mariya was looking at him. Her face held fear; it also held the furious concentration with which she assigned people to work when a roof leaked or a child was hurt. She pointed at the near stair. The twelve had to get through before the Iwang reached their opening.

“Go,” she called. “Get them out.”

He wanted her to ask him to stay. He wanted her to let him save her first, because then he would not have to decide whether saving the twelve mattered. She gave him a task instead.

***

They brought the twelve into the yard in groups of three. Villagers took them down the lane to rooms already prepared by Mariya's coastal network. Jorra held the passage until the last person cleared it. Panjios closed the near stair field; it kept the Iwang from following but left the far chamber connected to the root. The black threshold beneath the old steps pulsed once, then settled into a dangerous stillness.

Tobious could see Mariya through a narrow inspection pane. She had gathered the people nearest her around the dry recess. Nerissa was checking Annet's shoulder. Lios sat with his back to the wall, cup between his knees. He raised it when Tobious looked in, a tiny sign that he was still himself. Mariya touched the wafer Ypin had given her. A brief mark appeared on its edge.

“It has a relay window,” Ypin said. “If she records something, we can get it.”

“How soon?”

“When the diagnostic line opens. I can't make it open while this branch is carrying them.”

Panjios ordered the external chamber locked against unauthorized labor. Darran argued that his workers needed to continue the survey. Panjios showed Therren the load map and the rising strain. The captain had lost one guard in the first opening; he was not eager to lose more. He permitted the site to be held until specialist instructions came, though his men remained at the outer door.

Suss repeated the withdrawal order. Tobious knew enough now to see why Panjios obeyed it. Staying with Severance in front of an unmapped field would give him another chance to make a cut that harmed people he could not see. He also knew that obedience did not erase the choice he had already made.

At Salt Gate, the freed twelve were counted into Mariya's local shelter register, not transported to Veyr. The team crossed without them. When the physical gate closed behind Tobious, he held the sword by its worn grip and remembered his own words to Mariya in the fig shade: *I won't decide what you can bear because I have a blade now.*

He had decided for forty-eight people. Twelve others were breathing free air because of it. Both facts belonged to him, and neither answered the other.
