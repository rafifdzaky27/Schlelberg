# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Twenty-Seven — A Gate for Everyone

On Day Eighty-two, the Salt Gate opened onto sea air Tobious knew.

He stepped through after Jorra and before Sennos. Ypin remained at the future controls. His voice reached them through a narrow paired relay, its window measured in minutes. Edda's emergency crew held the Veyr side under the civil access order; the marshals there could keep a corridor open, not win a battle on the ancient shore. The three of them carried shields and blades. Tobious also carried the old knowledge of where the path flooded when the tide turned.

From the salt cave to Aras, the fields looked smaller than he remembered and the ridges steeper. It was the same landscape. He had been living among its future bones long enough to misjudge the distance between an olive tree and a wall. Jorra kept her shield faint around the group, ready but not wasting charge. Sennos checked the stretcher straps brought through in bundles.

On the first terrace a boy was gathering fallen olives into a reed tray. He froze at the sight of the shields. Tobious recognized him only after the boy turned; his father had shared a wall with Tobious's family. Eighty-two days had sharpened his face. The boy set down the tray and ran toward the village, not to announce a hero but to warn the households that armed strangers had come from the cave.

The harvest had been taken in measures too small to empty the fields, enough to leave the trees tended and the people short. Tobious saw the occupation in the cleared threshing place where carts waited under a Telassari seal, in the repairs made to the well with a kind of stone Aras had never chosen, and in the road kept smooth for a tax wagon while the footpath to the houses washed away. He had wanted to return with a sword powerful enough to make the village whole. The village had spent his absence surviving in particular ways he had not been there to learn.

Therren's soldiers met them above the shore path. The captain stood behind six spears and a hide shield, his red cloak dusty at the hem. One of his men pointed at Severance's case.

“No armed party enters Aras,” Therren said.

“Your surveyor has forty-five people under the old storehouse,” Tobious answered. “We have a route to bring them out.”

“Darran says moving them destroys the works.”

“It will end his commission.”

The captain's eyes narrowed. Tobious did not ask him to become kind. He had another kind of pressure behind him: coastal carriers waiting at the harbor with loaded carts, unwilling to move them until the village laborers were released. The captain's own soldiers needed the road kept open. Some had been sent to work the Receiver and knew Darran had concealed what was below.

One of those soldiers said, “My cousin was sent down there with a water crew. Darran says he was transferred.”

Therren turned to him. “You will speak when asked.”

The soldier lowered his gaze but did not withdraw the question. Tobious did not know whether the cousin was among the forty-five. He would not claim the man was alive to buy passage. “Mariya has a roll,” he said. “If you let the crews out, you can ask her.”

Behind the spears, a second soldier shifted his feet. The line Therren commanded was not one mind. Some feared the field shields, some feared losing their posts, and at least one wanted a name more than he wanted an order. Tobious kept Severance in its case. Drawing it would tell all six men that their only choice was to fight or yield.

One soldier advanced to block the path. Tobious lifted his field, and the spear point met a force it could not pierce. He did not draw Severance. He stepped sideways, leaving the man room to retreat without being thrown. Jorra and Sennos held position behind him, shielding the medical supplies.

“I can hurt your men,” Tobious told Therren. “I don't need to, and you need them to keep the ridge. Let us pass for the people Darran calls his workers.”

The captain looked toward the harbor. A carrier woman spoke from beside her cart: “If he stops them, we unload here. The ridge can feed itself.”

Therren knew the threat was imperfect; the villages needed trade too. It was still costly enough that he let the three pass under escort. He ordered his men to stay outside the Receiver unless called to carry a patient. It was a concession under pressure, not a treaty. Tobious took it and moved uphill.

At the last cart, the carrier woman caught his sleeve. Her palm smelled of rope and salt. “If you bring them through,” she said, “we can take the wounded down the west road. The ridge patrol hasn't barred it.”

“Can the carts turn at the ancestor terrace?”

“One at a time. No more. We'll keep the others below.”

He passed that constraint to Sennos. One cart at a time would slow the outside transfer, but the carriers had already chosen where to wait and how to move. They were part of the rescue before anyone in Veyr had entered their names on a plan.

Samon waited at the ancestor terrace with a cane and his left leg secured in its brace. He had laid out wedges, stretcher boards, and lengths of rope with the mason, two millers, and Lios's aunt. He did not ask Tobious what Veyr had made of him. He pointed to the old drain.

Lios's aunt had brought a second cup, its rim unglazed and uneven. “He said you'd know the first,” she told Tobious. “He asked me to keep a spare in case he dropped it.”

The first cup had already become a signal under the storehouse. Tobious took the second and set it beside the stretcher boards. A child's preparation could not hold back the Iwang. It could keep the adults from depending on a single fragile thing.

“If you open the far branch, that channel takes the water,” he said. “It will reach the holding recess first.”

Tobious crouched at the grate. The flow was already heavier than the plan's model had predicted. A wall repair since his departure had diverted runoff into the old passage. On Ypin's future map the channel was dry; in Aras, the last rain had changed it.

“We can shift it west before the first cut,” Tobious said.

Samon nodded at the mason. She brought two wedges and a board. The workers opened a narrow side channel, directing the water toward a basin that had once caught overflow from the granary. Tobious helped hold the board while the mason set the wedges. The wet edge slipped against his palm; Samon saw it and put a younger worker at that end without making it a comment on his courage.

The basin filled. The holding recess remained dry. A diagram from Veyr had given them the route; people who lived above it had made the route real.

Samon tapped the retaining board with his cane. “It will hold while the basin stays lower than this seam. When the tide comes in, someone must watch the level.”

The mason volunteered, then named a miller to relieve her. Tobious began to offer Sennos's monitor, but the monitor measured field load, not water. He let the mason mark the safe depth with a strip of charcoal and put a pot beneath the overflow to make the rising water audible. They had built a warning a tired person could hear without staring at a line.

“Your leg?” Tobious asked Samon while the crew reset the board.

“Attached.”

“That was not what I meant.”

“I know.” Samon shifted weight onto the cane. “It hurts when I make the mistake of being twenty again. Today I intend to be my actual age and tell other people where the stones go.”

Tobious had carried a memory of Samon crushed beneath the beam. The man before him was neither restored nor finished. He was giving orders to a mason who disagreed with him, and both of them were keeping a passage dry.

Jorra checked the outer passage for field pressure. Sennos set a shield stand where a stretcher could turn. Through the inspection pane, Tobious saw Mariya with Nerissa and Orran. Her hair was tied back with cloth worn thin at the ends. She raised her hand when she saw him. He did not ask her to leave her station and come to him. The plan was alive because she was standing where she had chosen to stand.

For the length of that raised hand he saw the woman from the night before he left, measuring seed by a lamp and refusing to say she trusted a promise she could not verify. Now she stood inside the danger he had made worse. He wanted to put his forehead against the pane. Instead he raised his own hand, then pointed to the dry recess and waited until she nodded. The gesture carried no apology large enough for the weeks between them. It carried the place where the first stretcher could be set down.

Ypin's voice came through the relay. “Low-load interval beginning. I can hold the first branch reading. After each cut, confirm the far floor before you touch the next.”

Mariya gave the signal from inside. Lios's cup rose once from the dry recess, with Nerissa's hand visible behind it. The fourth ration bell had sounded. Orran used the copied latch tool to open the ordinary service panel. It yielded without stirring the temporal field. Darran's guard was still at the ration hatch, surprised that a panel he had ignored could matter.

Tobious drew Severance. The blade's pale metal remained dull until it met the near tether. Jorra took position at his exposed side. He waited for Mariya's second signal that the first stretcher and its bearers were clear of the seam. When it came, he set the edge and counted three heartbeats.

The first beat drew the tether tight enough that the stone under his boot seemed to pull away. On the second he felt the shield fall from his own shoulder, leaving the skin of his neck aware of every grain of dust in the passage. Jorra's field held at his side. On the third the blade did not flash. A line that had been too thin to see became visible by ceasing to bend the air. He had once imagined a heroic sword announcing itself in light. Severance made room for a person to move, and demanded that he know who moved first.

The line parted. His own shield dropped; Jorra's field took a spray of stone from the wall. Inside, the first group could move. Orran and another worker guided a stretcher toward the wide recess. Nerissa read the names aloud. Tobious did not start the next cut. The core had to settle, and the people already freed needed to reach cover before a different branch could take their load.

On the future-side readout, Ypin saw a temporary rise. “Within the plan,” he said. “Wait.”

Waiting had never felt so much like work. Annet had died while he trusted a faster setting to make time for everyone. He could hear the baffle clicks and the feet of people turning a stretcher. The thirty seconds passed without any new cut. He asked Mariya whether the holding recess was clear.

“Not yet,” she said. “Hessah's bearers need one more turn.”

Sennos looked at the charge left in the corridor shield. They could speed the second group by leaving the slowest section behind for one interval. The choice appeared on the map without being named. Tobious touched the relay.

“We hold,” he told Ypin. “No bypass.”

Mariya heard him. “Move the second board toward the recess,” she said. “The bearers can rest there without blocking the first exit.”

Samon's crew shifted it from the outer stair. The adjustment came from the people who could see both the old passage and the bodies inside it. Tobious let them change his sequence. The core was ready before the people were; he waited for them.

At the far wall, dust began to cross a seam against the direction of the draft. A mineral click sounded beneath the baffle cycle. Jorra lifted her shield toward it. The first stretchers were moving, the rescue was real, and an Iwang had found the fresh current that made the passage possible.

“Hold the next group,” Jorra said.

Mariya repeated the order inside. The bearers lowered the stretcher in the dry recess they had measured. Nobody tried to run the turn while Jorra's field hid it from view. The Iwang pressed a pale limb along the active wall. It widened where the seam narrowed, then gathered weight at the collar. Sennos met it with his shield from the side, forcing its solid body away from the patient rather than driving it toward the other branch. The impact sent dust into the water basin Samon's crew had placed beneath the overflow. The basin rang. The channel still held.

Tobious waited for the creature to enter Severance's reach. Jorra did not call him forward. The blade had settled, but a cut here would open another line before the people in the recess could move. He remembered the guard's spear at the first opening and how its bronze point had found the collar only when the Iwang was solid. Jorra knew that limit too. She used an ordinary field blade to keep the creature from pressing past the shield, then withdrew when it thinned into the boundary. She had not killed it. She had made the passage unavailable for that interval.

Outside, the carrier woman placed one cart at the west-road turn where the first people to reach open air could be loaded. The second waited below, empty enough to turn when called. Samon's mason relieved the watcher at the drain. In the annex Nerissa read the living roll again, not because it had changed but because people needed to know whose movement the next cut depended on. Ypin's relay gave them the low trough that would continue into morning. The team agreed to hold the staged group under cover while the branch settled into that quieter state. No one left the far floor without Mariya's signal.

Tobious set the sword across his knees on the outer stair, where he could see the dry recess and hear the old watercourse. Sennos took the first watch. Jorra checked her heated shield unit and made the reserve ready. The Iwang clicked somewhere beyond the wall whenever the current rose. Dawn would bring the longer opening they needed. Waiting through the night was part of using it without asking the slowest people to pay for everyone else's haste.
