# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Twenty-Two — No More Names

On the first night after Panjios died, Tobious laid Severance on the floor between himself and Ypin.

The room belonged to a family in the lower service ward who knew Ypin from repairs. They had given them the narrow sleeping space behind their kitchen, two blankets, and a warning that Suss's search teams had already come through the adjacent street. Edda had sealed the cut beneath Tobious's left eye and dressed Ypin's forearm before sending them there by a side route. She had said the wound would scar. Tobious had not asked whether it matched the face he had once seen in the impossible opening.

At supper the family's youngest child asked why the visitors slept behind a curtain if they were not sick. Her father said some people were looking for them. The child considered the answer and asked whether those people would look in her room. Tobious wanted to tell her no. He could not. Her father said they had a rule for visitors: when someone knocked after dark, the children stayed with their grandmother and the adults answered together. It was not a guarantee. It was a household choosing how much of the danger it could bear, with its own children in the room.

Tobious thanked them and offered to leave. The grandmother pointed to the bowl she had set for him. “Eat first,” she said. “Then ask me again in the morning when neither of us is trying to decide while hungry.” He ate. They let him stay. No one called it safe.

“Take it,” he said.

Ypin looked at the blade. Its pale edge caught a thin line of light from the kitchen. The opaque core remained whole. It could still do the work for which generations had kept it.

“Where?” Ypin asked.

“To Suss. Tell him I'll stand down if he gets Mariya and the others out.”

“He has offered to take Mariya. He hasn't offered the others a safe route.”

“He needs the reference. He might bargain.”

“He needs you to agree to a cut. He cannot get that from me carrying a case into his office.”

Tobious had known the answer. He wanted Ypin to refuse it aloud so he could hate the refusal instead of the hope that had made him ask.

“If I hold it again,” he said, “someone else will pay.”

“They may. You also left a spike unreported. Those are not the same sentence.”

Tobious turned his head. The skin beneath his eye pulled against Edda's dressing. “You knew I was afraid of losing the release window.”

“I did not know you had proof of a Storehouse draw in your own hand.”

Ypin's voice had gone quiet. It was worse than shouting. He had abandoned the best routing trace to free Tobious under a falling rail, and now he sat across from a man who had kept an earlier trace because looking at it might interrupt a plan.

“You were with me after the shutter,” Ypin said. “I asked whether the line returned to the artificial sink. You said Suss had requested confirmation. I thought you had shown me all you had.”

“I let you think that.”

Ypin turned the burned side of his arm away from the stove. “I cannot work with another version of that answer.”

Tobious wanted to promise he would never hide anything again. The promise would be too large and too easy. “You can see the file now,” he said. “Edda can copy it. Put my date beside it.”

“I will.”

Ypin did not move toward the sword. Tobious understood that letting the file be copied was the beginning of repair, not its reward.

“I should have told you,” Tobious said.

“Yes.”

“You still came back for me.”

“You were under metal.”

Neither of them used the act as payment for the other one's failure. Ypin wrapped his burned arm more tightly and went to the kitchen for water. Tobious left the blade where it lay until morning.

***

The next days did not have the shape of a decision. Tobious slept in short pieces. He woke reaching for the manual lever Panjios had held, then remembered the door had come down. Once he set out a third cup while the family shared their evening broth. He had brought a third cup to Ypin's table for weeks, expecting some colleague might visit. Now the empty cup made him think of the older man's habit of asking one more practical question before letting a lesson end.

The grandmother moved the extra cup to the drying shelf without asking whose place it marked. Later she brought it back filled with water when Ypin's dressing needed changing. Tobious watched her use the thing he had turned into a symbol. Panjios had been more than the man at the door and more than the man who signed the old closure. The cup could be used. It could not hold an answer from him.

He was angry at Panjios for dying before he could answer more of them. The anger did not keep him from missing him.

Edda visited through the back entrance. She examined the scar and made him follow her finger with his eyes. The left side of his vision remained clear. His right wrist had begun to shake when he held a tool too long; she could not yet say how much came from the bypass, exhaustion, or injury. She gave Ypin a different dressing for the forearm and told him the burn would slow fine work while it healed.

Outside, the Concord notices continued to name Tobious as the cause of the Day-57 failure. Some residents believed them. Others had been rescued at the cooling station and asked why the report said nothing about the people whose bodies had carried the root. Edda collected their statements. A statement was not a shield against a search team; it might become one part of an order the city could enforce.

The host came in one evening with a notice copied onto his work slate. A neighbor had asked whether keeping Tobious in the ward put the water line at risk. The neighbor had lost a brother to the skyway repairs years earlier and trusted Suss's command more than a stranger from Aras. Tobious offered to speak to him. The host refused for now. The neighbor had asked a fair question about his family's exposure, and a confrontation on the doorstep would make the host answer it in public before he had chosen what to say.

“I told him you helped at the cooling shutter,” Ypin said.

“Did you tell him about the spike?”

“I told him the investigation is incomplete. The dated file should be in Edda's hands, and when you testify, that part has to be yours to say.”

Tobious could have asked Ypin to speak for him. He had already made the technician carry too much of his concealment. Some neighbors accepted the explanation. Others did not. The family kept the curtain closed at night.

On the third day, Tobious asked whether she knew how many captives remained.

“No verified count since Mariya's last delivered packet,” she said. “Forty-seven then. We have a sign of a failed transmission after that, not its contents.”

He had seen the Storehouse spike. He thought of Mariya recording an injury and receiving only a broken light. He did not say that she must be alive. He wanted to believe it without making Edda certify the wish.

“What would Suss do if I surrendered?”

“He would use your reference if he could persuade you. He would keep the ancient load until his infrastructure plan was secure. I have seen nothing that changes that.”

“He can pull one person out.”

“Possibly. Ask yourself whether you are offering him your cooperation or offering Mariya a choice she has already refused.”

Edda did not tell him what he felt. She returned to the clinic. Tobious sat with the question until he could no longer make surrender sound like an act of mercy performed for someone else.

***

On Day Sixty-one, Ypin's host came home with a water pressure map. The manual reservoir above the service ward was losing flow into a closed maintenance basin. Normal crews had been reassigned to root security, and searches made the access roads unreliable. Households at the lower end of the ward had begun saving drinking water in cooking pans.

Ypin spread the map on the kitchen table. “I can't lift the manual collar with this arm,” he said. “You know what water does when a lower channel silts.”

Tobious looked at Severance in its case. “The sword won't help.”

“I didn't ask for it.”

They went with the host and a reservoir worker who knew the basin's valves. The path was hot beside the service vents. People had set containers outside their doors; one child had written a name on each, so the neighbors would know whose water had been fetched. Tobious followed the pipe marks to the basin and found where a gate had not seated against its lower lip. The map showed an open return. The water was taking the easier path into the closed basin.

“Stop the upper feed,” he said. “We'll clear the lip and reset the collar.”

The reservoir worker held up a hand. “The upper feed cools two blocks. We can slow it. We cannot stop it long enough for you to climb inside.”

Tobious had known a village sluice that tolerated a complete halt. This city did not. He asked for a diversion board. They found one in the service store, fitted it over part of the channel, and let water continue through the cooling line while the remaining flow dropped below the lip.

Ypin checked the powered indicators with his unburned hand. Tobious used his left to scrape packed mineral dust from the seating groove. The right hand shook when he tried to hold the collar. He had enough strength to lift it an inch, not enough to keep it there while the worker reached the latch.

“Set it down,” she said. “Let me take that side.”

He did. She took the load with a short bar, and he guided its angle from the water's movement. Ypin called the moment when the gauge matched the physical flow. The worker seated the latch. Water began to fill the lower ward line.

No one applauded. The host thanked the worker for making the access safe and went home to check the household containers. Tobious washed grit from his hands. At the first downstream tap, a boy filled a kettle without waiting for the pipe to cough air. He did not know Tobious had been there.

The water reached the houses before anyone thought to thank him. Tobious was glad it did not have to pass through his name.

***

On Day Sixty-two, Edda admitted Tobious and Ypin through the clinic's side door again. She had kept the medical records and the partial archive copies safe. She had also asked three service supervisors whether they would back a civil emergency review. One would testify to the interrupted loads. Another wanted a complete trace first. The third feared Suss's field authority and would say nothing.

“I can treat you here,” Edda said. “I can preserve what you witnessed. I cannot ask marshals to seize a root hall while I have no release sequence for the captives and no backup for the ward's water and transit.”

“We can build one,” Tobious said.

“Then bring me the living count, a path a stretcher can take, and the load each cut moves. Tell me where these residents go while the root is severed. If any of those answers is missing, I will say so.”

He looked at Ypin. The technician's burned arm rested across his lap. They had enough evidence to know the current arrangement was wrong. They did not yet have enough capacity to replace it without hurting people.

“Tomorrow,” Tobious said. “I'll be at the planning room.”

It was a small promise. He could keep it even if the next answer frightened him. Severance remained in its case at the shelter. He had not surrendered it to Suss, and he had not drawn it to make the room agree with him.
