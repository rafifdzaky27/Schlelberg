# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Twenty-One — What Power Takes

The operation began with forty-seven names the public board refused to show.

Ypin had written them on a private strip beside the control desk. Mariya's latest packet had not come through, so the list still held Pehl and Annet. Tobious knew they might be alive. He also knew the last field surge had registered from the Storehouse branch without a clinical report. He told himself the missing packet was another delay of the old kind, not a sign that the people beneath Aras had been hurt while Veyr prepared its machines.

Suss brought the team to an active branch control room beneath Root Hall. Behind a transparent wall, the root's light moved through a narrow seam in ancient stone. From here Tobious could see the city above through layered service walks: the High Concourse, its skyway, and the transit lines carrying people to work. The root had been enclosed by generations of repair. It was old; the need for it was current.

A repair shift was finishing when they arrived. Its supervisor had a child's drawing folded into the pocket of her work coat, the paper soft at the creases. She asked Suss whether the corridor would remain open after the test; her crew had a damaged cooling joint to replace before night. Suss gave her a time and asked her to move the two workers nearest the branch to reserve cover. She obeyed because he had kept such promises before. Tobious had seen him protect crews under pressure. That history made this room harder to reduce to a struggle between an obvious villain and everyone who could see through him.

The plan called for one high-gain cut at a rated support line. The Storehouse captives would remain tethered while engineers tested a release sequence. Panjios insisted on a manual isolation door standing ready. Suss granted it but placed command staff at its controls.

Ypin sat at the trace station with the full routing view finally opened for live operation. He checked the artificial sink first, then the return path. His face changed. Tobious had learned to notice the moment a maintenance person stopped seeing numbers and started seeing what a number could do.

“The return isn't isolated,” Ypin said. “It goes through biological stabilization.”

“Rated below injury,” Suss replied. “Keep the sequence.”

Ypin expanded the line. There were identifiers beside the draw, not just a total. He cross-matched the first with Mariya's roster. Nerissa. The second with Hessah. Others followed, people whose names Tobious had heard over the relay. The label *artificial load* sat above them. Beneath it, the root would take the cost from living bodies.

Ypin called the numbers aloud to the supervisor. She went to the wall display and found the same return path under her crew's permit, but without the biological identifiers. Her hands were still dirty from the joint repair. “My people were told it was isolated,” she said.

“The local sink is,” Suss answered. “The stabilizing return is not.”

“Then they were told half the route.”

Suss did not deny it. He ordered her crew back from the control line, arguing that an interrupted root surge would kill people in the ward above. The supervisor moved her workers to cover but remained where she could see the tracing. She was not joining Tobious. She would be able to tell someone what the room had shown.

Tobious took out his private device. The spike from Day Fifty-three matched the return signature. He had seen this shape before and left it in the dark because the method had saved Ypin. Now a line from the earlier day ran into the same group of names.

“I had a spike,” he said. “After the cooling shutter. I didn't file it.”

Ypin turned toward him. He was not surprised that the anomaly existed. He was surprised that Tobious had held it.

Suss pointed at the rising load. “If you stop now, the future service line loses its support. We finish the mapped cut, then compensate.”

“You called it an artificial sink.”

“That chamber has one. The return path uses the network. We have kept it under the tolerances.”

“Thesia died. Two more may have died after the last surge. Your tolerance doesn't have their names.”

“You don't know what happened to the two.” Suss's answer was exact and cold. “I know what happens to the service ward if you release this line halfway through.”

Panjios moved toward the manual door. “We can isolate, take the city load on reserve, and stop until the names are checked.”

“Reserve will not hold the full corridor,” Suss said. “You signed the operational risk.”

“I signed a rated artificial return.”

The root's tone rose. Tobious felt it in his right hand where he held Severance. The first contact point waited ahead of him. He could make the cut Suss wanted, and another few people in Veyr might keep water and transit today. In Aras, people with names would take a cost they had never accepted. He set the blade down on the insulated stand.

The metal met the stand with a sound smaller than the root's rising tone. Tobious had imagined refusal would feel like a door closing against Suss. Instead he saw the repair supervisor still checking her crew, Ypin trying to preserve the names, Jorra watching the exposed seam. The choice to stop had left them all inside the danger. He would have to help them leave it without using another blind cut.

“I won't make the next cut,” he said.

Suss crossed the room without a threat or a speech. He reached for the sword and Tobious's wrist at once. He could not sustain the reference contact by forcing Tobious's hand, but he could hold him in place while command drove the gain and the root continued to draw. Tobious pulled free. The control rail shuddered. A lens above the seam cracked and fell.

His shield had thinned during the aborted contact. A shard cut below his left eye. Blood entered the corner of his vision. He raised the injured hand too late to stop the next piece. Jorra's field caught it; she was forced backward into the door frame.

“Isolation,” Panjios called.

He seized the manual control. The heavy door began to descend between the active branch and the service walk. Two future workers were still on the far side, trapped after the rail shifted. Ypin went for them while Sennos covered the passage. The root made a sound like water forced through a pipe too narrow to hold it.

Panjios held the isolation control with both hands. The mechanism required pressure from someone present; if he released it before the workers crossed, the door would drop into their path. His old knee shook under the load. Tobious could see the service descent beyond him, a set of narrow steps cut between the newer machinery and the ancient wall. Ypin had shown him the route on ordinary maintenance visits. It was the way out if the powered lifts failed. Today they had.

The supervisor with the drawing reached the near side of the door. She called the trapped workers by name. One answered; the other was trying to free his boot. Panjios asked her to take the first down the service steps and keep the landing clear. She wanted to stay for both. He said the door could only hold while people moved through it. She looked at his knee, then took the first worker's arm and led her away. The drawing showed at her pocket as she went.

“Come with us,” Tobious said.

“When the last one is through.”

The first worker reached the gap. Sennos pulled her clear. The second had caught a boot beneath a bent coupling rail. Ypin braced the rail and tried to lift it. Tobious moved to help. His right hand trembled; blood from beneath his eye spotted the stone. He set Severance's inert flat under the rail as a lever. It shifted enough for the worker to drag his foot free.

“Now,” Tobious said to Panjios.

The older man looked at the door. He would have to release the control and cross before its weight came down. Tobious reached for his arm, believing he could pull him in one motion. Panjios tried. The rail sprang against the worker's path. He pushed the control down again and the gap held.

“Get them out first,” he said.

It was the same choice he had denied ninety-seven others twelve years ago. Tobious saw it and refused to let the resemblance make this death righteous. Panjios could have told the truth earlier. He could have helped stop the current loading before the room shook. He was helping now, and help was not a way to erase the record.

Suss reached the control from the command side. “Release it. We can reroute the corridor.”

“Through the ancient branch?” Panjios asked.

“Through the rated network.”

“The network has names.”

Suss drew his field blade. Tobious saw the beginning of its coupling at Panjios's shoulder. Jorra was still holding cover over the workers. Sennos was on the descent with the first. Tobious took one step toward Suss, but the bent rail dropped in front of him. He had no clean path through it and no shield strong enough to force one.

Panjios could have let the control go and defended himself. He held the door long enough for Ypin to push the second worker through. Suss's blade passed the shoulder sector of his field. The older man fell against the manual lever. The door stayed open a little longer under his weight.

Tobious had seen Panjios use that same shoulder to demonstrate a shield turn on the village terrace. The motion now was smaller, the body unable to keep pace with the blade. There was no noble posture to make the blow easier to watch. Suss had chosen a place the defender could not guard without letting the door fall. The second worker stumbled across the line and Sennos caught him. Only then could Sennos clear the last boot from the path.

“Panji!” Tobious called.

Panjios turned his head. Tobious did not know whether he heard the familiar name or merely the sound. Then Suss pulled the lever clear. The door came down between them.

For a moment Tobious saw only the dark metal panel. It was thick enough to keep a surge from the escape passage. It was not thick enough to make the man on the other side less dead.

Ypin grabbed Tobious's coat. “The descent. Now.”

“The archive—”

“We have partial copies. We don't have another door.”

He had connected a recorder to the live trace. It held the complete routing view, including the names. He went back for it when the panel began to spark. The coupling rail fell across Tobious's leg. Tobious cried out and tried to pull free. Ypin looked once from the recorder to him, then abandoned the device.

He lifted the hot rail with a service bar. The heat burned his left forearm through the sleeve. Tobious dragged his leg clear. The recorder disappeared beneath a spray of glass as the active branch overloaded. The authenticated excerpts Ypin had copied earlier remained in his case; the strongest proof had been left because a living person was under metal.

They descended the service stair. Jorra and Sennos held the first landing while the two workers moved down. Tobious limped between them with Severance sheathed, unable to go back through the closed isolation door. Behind the wall, Suss ordered the Quarry access shut to prevent another surge. It answered with a series of violent pulses; its service channel would not carry a safe crossing now.

At the lower ward, emergency notices appeared on public surfaces. The Concord field commander reported that an unauthorized interruption by the new reference had threatened essential services. It was a partial account with enough truth to travel quickly. Tobious had delayed a warning, violated the Day-42 inspection limit, and refused the current cut. The notice omitted the names routed beneath the artificial-load label and Panjios's death at Suss's hand.

Ypin read it while Edda's clinic staff took the injured workers inside. His forearm was blistering. Tobious pressed a cloth beneath his left eye and found blood still coming through.

“How many are still there?” he asked.

“I don't know,” Ypin answered. “They're still there.”

“And Panjios—”

He could not finish. He had wanted the older man to answer for the ninety-seven, then to live long enough to help the people under Aras. Suss had taken the second possibility from him. That did not give Tobious back the first.

Edda opened a side entrance to the clinic. She looked at their wounds, then at the notices spreading above the street.

“Inside,” she said. “We can argue about who did what while I stop you bleeding.”
