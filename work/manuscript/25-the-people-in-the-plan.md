# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Twenty-Five — The People in the Plan

The first rescue failed in a room with no captives.

Ypin had marked the floor with tape for the annex walls, the ninth stair, and the wider service recess. Weighted frames stood in for those who could not walk. Jorra and Sennos brought shield stands; Edda brought two stretcher teams who knew what a body did when frightened, weak, or unconscious. Tobious set Severance's inert practice counterpart beside the painted branch line and waited for Mariya's recorded three-click interval.

Before they began, Edda asked one of the carriers to lie on a frame. He had carried patients through a tower fire and thought he knew how to remain still. When the bearers lifted him, he caught the edge with his hand at the first sway. Their plan had no place for that hand. It would be inside the active field.

“Again,” he said, embarrassed.

“After we change the hold,” Edda told him. “In the annex, frightened people will reach for what they can touch. We design for the hand.”

They wrapped a second strap where a patient could grip it. Tobious thought of Hessah, who had asked for the water skin with the red knot. He had written *personal item* in the margin. Now he moved it into the center of her carry plan. It could give her hands somewhere to go; if she could not hold it, her mother could walk where she could hear her voice.

The first release went well. The second started before the blade's core would have settled in a live operation. In the simulation, Sennos had to keep an exposed passage covered while the first stretcher turned. When Jorra moved to protect the cut, the stretcher lost its shield. The people assigned as bearers stopped rather than pretending the error could be survived by moving faster.

“I'll take the next cut alone,” Tobious said. “Then Jorra can stay with them.”

Ypin shook his head. “The blade still needs thirty seconds. Taking it alone removes your cover without changing the clock.”

“I can use the high gain once.”

“No. We built this plan because that setting moved the cost to people under Aras. We don't call it safe because the danger makes waiting unpleasant.”

Tobious felt the room turn toward him. He had made the failed intervention, hidden the spike, and then come here to lead an evacuation. He could defend his suggestion until everyone was weary enough to agree. He put down the practice grip.

“Ypin is right,” he said. “The first sequence fails. Show me the holding space.”

Jorra moved the first stretcher into a dry recess wide enough for two bearers to wait. Sennos shifted a shield stand so its field reached both the exit and the recess for a short interval. The new route asked one group to pause while the core settled, then move on Jorra's signal. It also required someone inside the annex to tell them whether the corridor was actually clear. Lios's proposed cup signal would be backed by Nerissa. A child would not be the only point at which forty-five lives depended.

They ran it again. One weighted frame would not turn. Edda's bearer said the person on it would be scraped along the wall. Tobious had used Mariya's measured width correctly and forgotten that a stretcher needed room for the hands carrying it. They changed the grip, not the person. In the third run, both frames cleared without a shield-down interval on the exit.

They did not call the third run a success until the carrier on the frame tried to rise halfway through it. The bearers lowered him at the marked recess. Sennos held cover while Jorra used the stop signal, palm open beside her shield. Tobious kept the practice blade still. Ypin's timer counted thirty seconds before allowing another cut. By then the carrier had sat up and apologized.

“Don't,” Jorra said. “That is the first thing someone there will do when they see daylight.”

The carrier lay down again, but he left one leg outside the strap to imitate a patient who could not bear pressure on a wound. It changed the turn once more. They shifted the inner bearer half a pace back and tested whether the frame still cleared. In the real passage, half a pace would put a boot close to the ninth step. Sennos marked the floor and made Tobious walk it with the shielding hum at his shoulder. A mistake had become a distance his body could remember.

At the end, Edda asked who could halt a cut. Tobious named Jorra, Mariya, Nerissa, and the person nearest the branch if the floor moved unexpectedly. Ypin added the future-side load monitor. The right to stop became an actual signal each would recognize. Without it, consent would be a sentence in a meeting note.

They recorded water, carriers, counts, and exits beside each stage. The list made the rescue look less like a heroic charge. It made it possible to practice.

***

Future residents had to leave their homes before they knew whether Tobious could save the ancient ones.

Edda's civil order moved the households nearest the root's exposed service blocks into temporary rooms above the lower ward. Reservoir workers transferred cooling to conventional power; food-tower crews prepared for harvest loss if the root supply failed. Nothing about the move was effortless. A woman who sold greens from her window asked who would pay for the days she could not open. An older man refused to leave a room whose stairs he could manage because the temporary shelter's lift failed too often. Edda's team found him another place instead of calling him unreasonable.

Tobious carried crates with the evacuation workers. His right hand was not steady enough for the full weight, so he took the light end and let someone else guide the heavy one. The woman with the greens recognized him from the Concord notice.

The temporary rooms had clear walls that could turn opaque when a family wanted privacy. A boy kept pressing the control until his grandmother asked him to leave one side transparent. She wanted to see the harbor. From this height, the water appeared to pass beneath the suspended gardens, though Tobious had learned that the gardens rode on service ribs strong enough to hold entire markets. Their beauty depended on a great deal of hidden labor. A kitchen worker was carrying hot grain cakes down the hall. He had been sent to a shelter where half the arriving families would not know how to reach a food station; he handed out the first cakes before he unfolded his official list.

Tobious took one when the worker offered it. It tasted of pepper and a leaf he could not name. For a moment he was simply hungry in a city where someone had cooked too much on purpose. He wondered whether Mariya would have liked the sharpness. Then he saw a young woman at the next door measuring her mother's medicine against the light. The cooling delay could spoil it. Edda's clinic had promised a powered container, but the delivery had not reached this floor. Tobious carried the request down to the service team and waited until a worker brought the container up. When he returned, the grain cake had gone cold in his hand.

“You brought this trouble from your village,” she said.

“The root was here before I was.”

“You're the one asking me to go.”

“Yes.”

She waited for the rest of his defense. He had one he could have given: the names under Aras, the false counts, Suss's purge. They were true. They would not make her shelf of cut greens keep fresh in a borrowed room.

“The shutdown will interrupt this tier,” he said. “Edda's team will record the loss and arrange food access. I can't promise it will be repaid quickly.”

“Then carry the boxes without pretending that is payment.”

He did. Before they left, she showed a younger worker which plants would survive the move and which should be given away now. Tobious took two bundles to a neighbor. The woman did not thank him. She did not owe him gratitude for asking her to bear a real cost.

At the access checkpoint, three civic marshals arrived under Edda's issued review order. Their warrant preserved passage for medical and inspection crews. It did not give Tobious command of Root Hall or dissolve the Warden chain. Suss's staff challenged the order at the gate; the marshals held their ground while Edda's registrar produced the casualty certificates. Each person on either side could see whose authority they were invoking. The city had begun to divide openly over a policy that had once been hidden in a column.

The senior marshal did not wave them through. She asked Edda to name every person who needed access and the work each would perform. Tobious's name drew the longest pause. Suss's notice called him a compromised operator; Edda's certificate called him the only current reference. Both descriptions could be true. The marshal entered them together and issued a guarded passage permit, valid only while an independent witness watched the coupling. Tobious signed beneath it.

“If Suss challenges this in court?” he asked.

“Then he challenges the order in public,” she said. “That is one of the things it is for.”

***

Ypin's legitimate maintenance feed showed a new pattern on Day Seventy-two. Suss had moved energy toward the terminal control junction while ordering ancient receiver rations restricted to approved crews. The reduced food to Aras would weaken the captives before the release window. The same route could arm a one-sided purge of the ancient volume while preserving the future root.

Edda took the ration order to the civil supply office. Its clerk had never seen the inside of the ancient annex. He could see, however, that the order removed food from a registered service site without naming an alternative delivery. He refused to suspend a Warden command on Tobious's account alone. He asked for the captive roll.

Mariya's packet was there, with forty-five names and the injured marked for carrying. The clerk read the first page without speaking. Then he compared it with Suss's supply ledger, which still called the same people *unprotected load*. He stamped a limited continuation of water and food under emergency care rules. It would last until the review, not until everyone was safe. Edda thanked him for exactly what he had done, and sent a copy of his stamped order to the carrier network before the route could close.

Outside, Tobious asked whether Suss would obey.

“He may obey the paper and starve them by delay,” Edda said. “That is why we still need the workers who move the food.”

They found two dispatchers willing to mark departures and arrivals separately. One would not sign a public statement. The other said he would, if his shift partner's family could be moved out of the exposed ward first. Tobious had once imagined that evidence would unlock a door. It was beginning to look more like carrying water: necessary, heavy, and lost if no one stayed to watch the vessel.

“He knows we are preparing to sever it,” Ypin said.

“Can he fire the purge now?” Tobious asked.

“Not cleanly. He still needs your coupling for precise work, or he must let the field build until an uncontrolled stabilization takes its place. Either way he risks the root he wants to keep.”

Suss had not become careless because his opponents had found witnesses. He was preparing a second choice for the city to accept if the rescue failed: destroy the ancient site, keep the useful infrastructure, call the loss an emergency. The calculation would include Aras as terrain, not as households.

Jorra listened to the feed with her jaw set. She had served under Suss long enough to know when an order contained a fallback he had not yet named. “He will call the purge mercy,” she said. “He'll say a clean end was kinder than a failing floor.”

“Would it be?” Tobious asked. He did not mean to provoke her. He needed to know whether some part of that claim still held her.

“For someone watching a graph, perhaps. Not for a person still asking for water beneath it.” She pointed to the ration restriction. “This is how you make your graph look desperate enough to justify what you already want.”

She gave Edda her copy of the order. It bore the command mark that she had once used without question. Her hand stayed on the edge until Edda took it. Sennos did not tell her the choice erased the years she had served. He took over her watch at the gate so she could go to the families moved from the lower ward and explain what their protective barrier could actually do.

The coalition could not enter the Storehouse safely before the low-load trough on Days Eighty-two and Eighty-three. They used the intervening days to position stretchers, verify shelter with Mariya's carriers, rehearse baffle signals, and enforce the civil access order. Ypin sent the complete proposal on the next diagnostic window: the cut sequence, the medical limit, the Day-84 closure, and the fact that Mariya could cross to Veyr if she chose before the root was consumed.

Tobious listened to the packet once before it left. He had asked that her options be named plainly. He heard no request that she prove she loved him by selecting one. The relay accepted the message. Whether she would accept any part of the plan remained for her to say.
