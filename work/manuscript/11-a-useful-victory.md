# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Eleven — A Useful Victory

The trouble began at a cooling station that served a thousand homes.

Tobious knew the number because Ypin had made him look at the service map before they left Root Hall. It was easy to admire the pale water descending through clear conduits above the city. It was harder to imagine a thousand kitchens growing hot at once because one door inside the station refused to open.

At the site, steam had gathered beneath the ceiling. A maintenance team was trapped between two powered barriers. Their voices reached the operations desk through a narrow line; the images from that corridor broke into bands of light whenever the root load rose. An Iwang had flattened through a thinning seam beside the cooling return. It moved between the workers and the only manual exit.

Suss spread the station plan across the desk. He pointed out the route before assigning anyone to it. Jorra and Sennos would hold a shield line at the outer corridor. Ypin would identify the barrier seam from maintenance access. Tobious would make one guarded cut and then withdraw with the workers. Suss would cover the route behind them and isolate the return once the people were clear.

“One cut,” Suss said to Tobious. “If you see another opening, you report it. You do not follow it.”

Tobious nodded. The word *one* had been repeated enough that he could hear its purpose. No one was paying him to be brave twice.

They entered through a service walk that narrowed near the cooling pipes. Condensation slicked the floor. Below the grate, water ran at a force that would break a faller's bones. Tobious set his feet with care. Jorra touched his shoulder once, a signal that she had room to extend cover past him when his shield dropped.

The trapped workers had gathered behind the inner barrier. One sat with her hands over her ears. Another held a short metal bar as though it could matter against the thing in the corridor. The third had a cut on his cheek and was talking steadily to the woman, telling her what he could see beyond the door. None of them wore field protection.

“The Iwang is at the return bend,” Sennos said.

Tobious saw dust cross a seam in the wall. The clicking came a moment later. He remembered the open chest and the guard's spear beneath Aras. This creature's struts were paler, or the station light made them so. Its body narrowed as it pressed near the active boundary, then took shape again beside a live conduit. It was not looking for a duel. It was following a gradient toward the protected cluster of people.

“Seam?” Tobious asked.

Ypin crouched at a maintenance plate. “Third mark from the floor. The others belong to the cooling wall. Cut those and the water hits the corridor.”

Three marks looked almost alike. Tobious waited for Ypin to touch the right one with a short probe. He would have chosen the second.

The Iwang shifted. Sennos raised his field as it struck the bend. The impact drove him back a step. Tobious started forward, wanting to cut before the next blow. Jorra held him by the arm.

“On my line,” she said.

It was the same phrase she had used a dozen times on the practice floor. He felt embarrassed by the need to hear it. Then she moved, and his body followed the arrangement they had made.

He set Severance to the third mark. The channel darkened, then gathered its pale thread. One heartbeat. The Iwang's limbs scratched along Sennos's field. Two. Tobious wanted to rush the blade through. He kept contact instead, shifting one foot as Suss had taught him. Three. The barrier opened in a narrow band.

His own shield fell. Jorra's covered him from the bend. Ypin pulled the manual lever on the service plate; the divided panel swung away from the trapped team.

“Come through one at a time,” Tobious called.

The worker with the cut cheek helped the woman forward. She looked at the Iwang and froze. Tobious did not order her to be fearless. He reached through the opening with his left hand and showed her the grip she could take. She held on. He drew her into Jorra's cover, then turned for the others.

The Iwang struck again. Sennos's shield gave a warning pulse. The man with the bar pushed the cut worker through. He then backed toward the gap, refusing to turn his back on the creature. Tobious saw that he was too far from the exit for one pull. He wanted to go after him.

“Let him take two steps,” Ypin said. “The floor line is clear.”

The man stepped. Suss shifted the outer field to meet the next strike, leaving the Iwang's collar exposed for an instant. Tobious saw the dark nexus; Severance had not yet settled for another cut. He could have tried the ordinary edge if he reached beyond cover. The man with the bar was close enough now. Tobious took his sleeve, drew him through, and heard the panel shut behind them.

“Exit,” Jorra said.

They moved as a group. The woman who had been frozen now steadied the cut worker over the grate. The third worker kept his metal bar until the outer door closed, then set it down carefully, embarrassed by his own hand.

They had all come out.

Behind the sealed corridor the Iwang struck the return wall, then thinned as Suss dropped the local gradient. It retreated into the seam. Tobious watched until he was certain it could no longer reach them. Only then did he feel how wet his shirt had become under the shield band.

At the operations desk, Suss recorded the time and the one cut used. He did not call it a miracle. He asked Edda's team to examine the workers and ordered a repair crew to inspect the barrier before the cooling load returned. Two of the saved workers had families in the service ward; their names were entered as witnesses to the field fault.

The woman who had frozen at the opening asked whether her shift would be marked as abandoned. She had left her tools behind the barrier, and an abandoned shift would dock the pay that kept her mother's room heated. Suss told the registrar to mark it as a controlled evacuation. Tobious watched the woman check the entry herself before she let Edda's aide take her to the clinic. He had brought her through the door; the day's harm could still follow her home in a line of text if someone counted badly.

The worker with the metal bar wanted to return for it. “It belongs to the station,” he said, embarrassed that he had carried it like a weapon. Ypin promised to put it on the recovery list when the corridor was safe. Tobious had thought the man would want to forget the bar. He wanted to return a tool to a place where he still meant to work.

Jorra touched the edge of Tobious's wrist support. “You didn't follow it.”

“You told me not to.”

“Some people hear instructions as suggestions once they've succeeded at the first part.”

Tobious looked back at the service door. The room still held a thousand homes on its other side. Had he chased the Iwang and left the barriers failing, those homes would have remained at risk. The thought did not make his part small. It made its boundary clear.

***

After the workers were safe, Suss asked the team to stay for a controlled test.

The test load sat in an isolated chamber, made to resemble a receiver branch without any living anchor or open path to a settlement. Ypin checked the isolation marks twice. Panjios checked them a third time. Suss wanted Tobious to see a faster operating setting; he told everyone exactly what he would do before his hand went to the control.

“A guarded cut waits for the core to settle,” he said. “Thirty seconds is manageable here. If those three workers had been injured while the Iwang held the bend, thirty seconds might have cost one of them a leg.”

Suss adjusted the gain control and asked Tobious to place Severance against the artificial seam. Tobious kept the edge steady. A high-gain pulse crossed the chamber and opened the seam more quickly than before. The indicator above Suss's hand climbed. The reset lamp returned to green without counting a full thirty seconds. Heat rose through the floor.

“You can repeat it?” Tobious asked.

“In a mapped load. With a limit and a place for the strain to go.”

Suss had not said there was no cost. Tobious could see the station's cooling draw rise. The second pulse would take more. He could also see the usefulness. A trapped worker might live because a commander refused to wait for a safe clock.

Panjios moved closer to the readout. “The receiver carried the excess.”

“The artificial receiver,” Suss said. “With a rated sink.”

“There are live branches without rated sinks.”

“Then we map them before use.”

They held each other's gaze for a moment. Neither raised his voice. The disagreement belonged to the numbers on the screen, and to people Tobious could not see from this room.

“Would you have used it today?” Tobious asked.

“If the third worker had been caught behind the panel, yes,” Suss said. “I would have accounted for the heat after I got him out.”

Panjios turned to Tobious. “The word *after* needs somewhere real to put the cost. Ask for that place before you agree to a live cut.”

Tobious wanted to answer that a dying person could not wait for a perfect map. He had watched three people come out alive because they had been given one. He kept the thought; he did not yet know which man it supported.

***

Ypin's room in the lower service ward was narrow enough that two chairs blocked the cupboard if their occupants leaned back. Plants grew on a warm pipe by the window. A small domestic water unit sat on the table in pieces, waiting for Ypin to remember how it had fitted together.

From the window Tobious could see tiers of gardens cut into the sides of other buildings. A tram passed high above them without a rail he could see. Below, neighbors crossed a suspended footway carrying food in covered bowls to a family whose cooking line was out. The city could lift a road over the sea and still depend on someone deciding to take supper across a bridge. He had been too busy learning its dangers to notice how often its people made room for one another without a command.

“You repair pumps for a living,” Tobious said.

“This one is for tea. It has more rules.”

Ypin shoved the pieces into a bowl to make space for supper. He had made a dish of cultivated greens and fermented grain cakes, browned at the edges. Tobious took a bite. It was soft where he expected it to be crisp. The flavor pleased him less than Ypin's evident pride.

“Good?” Ypin asked.

“It wants salt.”

“It has salt.”

“Then it wants to be fried twice.”

Ypin made a wounded sound and pushed the pan toward him. Tobious used the little stove and turned the cakes until their surfaces caught. The smell changed. Ypin tried one and conceded that the extra heat had helped.

“At home,” Tobious said, “we would put onion between the layers. Mariya would tell me I had spent too much of it. Then she would take the crispest piece.”

“You say her name differently when you remember a meal.”

Tobious considered denying it. Instead he said, “I miss how she would ask whether I had thought about the next day's work. I hated the question when it came. Now I hear it while nobody is asking.”

Ypin had cleaned his plate. He set it by the broken water unit. “Sometimes I want a repair that is only a repair,” he said. “Fix a valve, go home, and let it stay fixed because I measured it correctly. No command route hidden behind a service panel. No category in a table whose contents nobody will let me check.”

Tobious looked at him. The last sentence was more specific than Ypin's ordinary complaints. Ypin shook his head before Tobious could ask.

“I haven't proved anything. I keep seeing categories I don't like.”

“Then let's see them together.”

“When I have the records.”

Ypin picked up the domestic unit, turned it twice, and set it back in the bowl. It was easier for him to make an uncertain machine the subject of his hands than an uncertain ledger the subject of his voice.

Tobious could have pressed. He wanted to celebrate the day while it was still intact: three people alive, a thousand homes cooling again, supper improved by a trick learned long before anyone had called him a reference. They ate the last cake between them.

On his way back through the service ward, Tobious passed a window where the city glowed beyond stacked roofs. He knew that if he sent Mariya a message, he would tell her they had brought people out. He would also tell her whose plan had worked and where he had needed another person's hand. She would ask what he had learned from it. He had an answer he could give without pretending to have learned everything.
