# SCHLELBERG

## Chapter Thirty — The King's Second Measure

Suss had set the purge to fire toward the ancient side.

The captives were out of the annex. That did not put them beyond the volume his command had marked for destruction. Aras, its fields, the salt shore, and the neighboring roads remained inside it. On the control wall, the region appeared as a pale shape without houses. Tobious thought of Therren's altered grain measure in the first days of occupation: a rim moved by one band could make a whole harvest vanish from the count. Suss's second measure was larger. It did not make the people beneath it less real.

Edda's three marshals held the access order at the Civic Spine checkpoint. Suss's field officers contested their right to enter the purge junction. Some had already read the names from Mariya's packet and the signed maintenance forms; one stepped aside after Tobious admitted his own concealed spike to the room. Another held his post, saying the ancient deaths were a command matter and the future city's services came first. The split did not end with a speech. It became a corridor with people on either side deciding whether to open a door.

Tobious had crossed Salt before dawn and come straight to the checkpoint. Mariya's wrist binding was still on his right arm. He could feel where she had tucked the loose end beneath the cloth. Across the barrier, a Warden officer kept his hand near the control that would seal the Root Hall approach. He was young enough that Suss might have trained him. Tobious recognized the stance even without knowing the man.

The first marshal read Edda's order aloud. It granted passage for a casualty review and protection of the service crews moving residents off root power. The officer answered with Suss's emergency field authority. Both seals were valid. Neither contained an instruction for what a person should do when two valid authorities demanded opposite things.

Edda presented Mariya's list beside the manifest's zero. A rescued worker from the cooling station confirmed the root's field effects and Suss's earlier rescue of her team. She did not invent an accusation about a man who had saved her. She said what she had seen and what she had since learned had been left out of the load record. Ypin showed his own signature on a classification form. Tobious supplied the trace he had concealed and said how long he had kept it.

The worker carried a small case that kept medicine cold for her mother. It had come from the lower ward's emergency power line. She set it on the checkpoint rail while she spoke. “I know the backup is imperfect,” she told the officer. “My mother's medicine is inside it. I helped test the line this morning. If it fails, I will be among the people hurt. I still will not sign a report saying the people under Aras were equipment.”

The officer asked Edda whether every exposed household had been moved. Edda said no. The ones nearest the root had; farther households were on independent cooling with manual checks, and some would lose food if the towers failed. She gave him the failure plan with its unanswered entries left visible. Suss had been right to name costs the city would carry. He had not gained the right to erase Aras by being right about them.

“So the reference admits he broke procedure,” the officer said.

“Yes,” Tobious answered. “Write that too.”

The man did not become a friend. He moved aside far enough for the marshals to pass. Another operator stayed at the door, refusing to join them. A third asked for the names to be sent to his own record before he would decide. Edda did it. The delay cost minutes they could see on Ypin's load gauge. It was still faster than asking people to abandon their judgment because Tobious held a famous sword.

“The purge preserves the root,” Suss said over the command channel. “If you sever it now, you take water and transit from people who never voted on your rescue.”

“They have been moved from the exposed tier,” Edda answered. “You were given the backup plan.”

“A backup that feeds a city for days. The root has kept it fed for generations.”

The argument deserved to be heard by the inquiry that would follow. It did not give Suss the authority to destroy Aras before that hearing. Ypin identified the purge path: an auxiliary surge route ran under the junction, feeding the ancient receiver volume while keeping the future root intact. If they cut that route before Suss could complete the command, the purge would fail. The root itself would remain overloaded and still need Tobious's final cut.

Jorra and Sennos took positions by the service rail. Ypin, his left arm still bandaged, timed the control pulses from a maintenance station he was authorized to read. Tobious waited with Severance behind their cover. He could hear the skyway traffic above them, a regular hum that would become dangerous if the root failed before evacuation finished.

Ypin put a second clock beside the load gauge. It measured the distance to the Day-84 peak, not the time until Tobious could safely use Severance again. Edda's last transit warning had slowed the skyway carriages but had not stopped every route; hospitals and service crews were still moving. Tobious understood why the city would resent what he was about to do even if he succeeded. No one had been able to evacuate an entire way of life in a few weeks.

“Three beats to couple,” Ypin said. “One route. Afterward, back to cover.”

Suss came through the far access with two loyal operators. He had never been a man to leave a difficult job entirely to others. His shield held steady as he approached; his blade traced Tobious's opening before Tobious made it. The first exchange proved what weeks of training had not changed: Suss knew distance better. Tobious would not win by matching his hands.

One operator called for Tobious to put down the relic. The other looked toward Ypin's station, measuring a path to the controls. Sennos gave them no clean angle. Jorra did not ask Tobious to defend her side while his own field fell. They had rehearsed the division: she would hold the center, Sennos the station, and Tobious would reach only the auxiliary line. The plan left Suss free to be the better fighter in a space where fighting was not the only objective.

Jorra shifted between them. Sennos blocked the operators from reaching Ypin. Tobious moved toward the auxiliary control as agreed. Suss anticipated it and used the junction's narrow rail to close the angle. Their blades met near contact. Tobious felt the pressure of Suss's field against Severance and kept his feet, but his own shield dropped as the relic sought a coupling line.

The floor was divided by maintenance channels covered in grating. Ypin had chosen this approach because it let a shield operator stand on either side of Tobious. Suss used the same channels to keep Tobious from stepping away when their blades touched. He feinted high, then pressed into the narrow sector near Tobious's right elbow. Tobious knew the correction Suss had taught him: move the foot, not the whole shoulder. He made the movement. It was just enough to keep the first edge from reaching him.

“You learned,” Suss said.

“You taught me.”

“Then listen now. If you cut that route, you leave this city dependent on a few temporary reservoirs.”

“We prepared them.”

“For days. The need lasts years.”

Tobious had no reply that could turn a damaged harvest into a small thing. He held his blade against Suss's and waited for Jorra's cover to cross his exposed side. She could not keep it there long; the loyal operator facing Sennos was pressing toward Ypin's station, where a broken control would end their timing.

Suss turned his edge beneath Tobious's guard. It passed his protection at the right side. Pain drove the air from his lungs. Tobious stepped back with blood warming his ribs, exactly where the man in the opening had held a hand. He had no time to study the resemblance. Jorra covered his retreat while Sennos moved the loyal operators away from the service route.

He pressed the wound and felt blood soak his tunic. Suss came after him one step, then stopped when Jorra's shield narrowed the passage. The commander did not need to finish him to win. He needed Tobious too hurt to complete a cut while the purge reached the ancient volume. Tobious could see the calculation in Suss's restraint, and hated how much it resembled the field lessons he had once admired.

“He is wounded,” Ypin said over the channel.

“I can stand,” Tobious answered.

“That is a different measurement.”

Jorra drew him into cover. Her shield unit warned of heat. Sennos could not replace her while the loyal operators held the other approach. Tobious tasted iron when he breathed and wondered whether Suss's blade had broken a rib or only laid the flesh open. Edda's medical team waited beyond the checkpoint; reaching them would mean leaving the surge route armed.

Ypin sent him a simple map rather than another speech. A maintenance pulse would take the auxiliary control out of Suss's direct reach for a short interval. Jorra could hold the middle. Sennos could keep the operators to the right. Tobious would have one path to the marked line and no cover if he chased Suss afterward. The plan assumed Suss would notice. It did not require him to forget how to fight.

“I can give you an earlier pulse,” Ypin said. “It risks the service station.”

“Use the measured one.”

Tobious felt the wrist binding Mariya had tied and steadied Severance in his left hand. He could still want Suss dead. He could still choose the route first.

Ypin gave him the next pulse window. Tobious pressed a hand to his ribs and counted the seconds until Severance's core had settled. The auxiliary route began to glow. Suss moved to intercept again; Jorra held him for one exchange, not long enough to defeat him but long enough to let Tobious reach the junction.

There was a moment when Suss's field thinned. Tobious could have turned after him. The blade was in reach of a human body that had killed Panjios and hidden the costs borne by Mariya's group. He felt the desire to make the story end with that cut.

Below them, the auxiliary route surged toward Aras. The forty-three survivors were in shelters along the shore and terraces. The shortest path to Suss would leave the control uncut for another pulse.

Tobious turned away from him.

The movement cost him the one line of protection Jorra could extend without exposing Ypin. She saw it and shifted her stance, risking the edge of Suss's field against her own shoulder. Sennos called that his operator had broken contact at the service rail. For three heartbeats all three of them accepted the openings the others had made. Tobious could not feel heroic inside it. His ribs made each breath smaller than the last, and his blade had to stay where the route lay.

He set Severance against the marked route. One heartbeat. His shield faded, and Jorra's field stretched between him and the commander. Two. The wounded ribs made it hard to keep the edge still. Three. The auxiliary line separated. Its light died before it reached the ancient volume.

Suss struck at his exposed side. Sennos met the attack, holding just long enough for Tobious to withdraw and his shield to return. Tobious could not have repeated the cut at once. He did not need to. The purge control went dark, and the command link that had protected Suss's access with it.

The loyal operators saw the line they had been ordered to preserve fail. One lowered his blade. The other hesitated until a marshal called the civil order through the open channel, then stepped back from the control. Two marshals entered with Sennos covering the doorway. Suss resisted the first attempt to take his weapon; Jorra caught his blade arm at the moment its field reset, and the marshals bound his wrists. The third secured the purge console and checked that no secondary sequence remained armed.

The senior marshal read the detainment basis into her recorder: active threat to an inhabited ancient volume, concealment of casualty evidence, and refusal of the independent access order. She did not call the charges a verdict. Suss asked whether she had verified the root would survive Tobious's planned cut. She said it would not, and ordered him held anyway. Her authority was narrow, but the threat in front of her fell within it. Suss's officers could challenge her account later with the same record she was making now.

No one in the room knew whether a second order had already been sent to the ancient side. Ypin checked the outgoing record against the line they had cut. The purge instruction had reached the auxiliary control but not passed the severed junction. A Salt diagnostic pulse returned from a standing receiver. It told them the route still existed; it could not show every house in Aras. The marshals kept the console sealed while a technician independent of Suss verified the remaining switches. Tobious did not leave until Ypin said the purge sequence was disarmed. The root load continued rising on the adjacent display.

Tobious stood apart, breathing shallowly. He had wanted to stop Suss since Panjios fell. When the man was taken past him alive, he felt no clean triumph.

“The city will count the days without the root,” Suss said. “Then it will know what you chose for it.”

“It should,” Tobious answered. “It should count the people you chose for Aras too.”

Edda's staff brought a pressure dressing for his side. They could keep him on his feet long enough to reach the seam; they could not make the wound harmless. The purge was stopped. The ancient shelters still stood. Above them, the skyway's hum faltered as accumulated overlap bent the high concourse. Ypin looked at the load gauge and did not ask Tobious whether he was ready. The measured deadline had arrived.

“Take me to the root,” Tobious said.
