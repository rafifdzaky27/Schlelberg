## SCHLELBERG — execution-ready story plan, version 1.1

### What this package is

A complete developmental blueprint for an English-language science-fantasy novel, designed in the current planning session for a separate execution model to draft without rebuilding the story. This is a plan, not a finished novel. One English chapter already exists. Chapter 2 was never written: two attempted continuation turns were interrupted before authoring.

The author discusses the project in Indonesian, wants critical collaboration, and wants drafts retained during development rather than receiving a whole chapter or output link after every turn. The author now explicitly requests detailed planning through the final chapter before resuming prose. Their intended execution model is Sol 6; no model switch is performed by this package.

### Authority and source hierarchy

1. Subsequent explicit author decisions override this package.
2. AUTHOR-LOCKED requirements below cannot be changed silently.
3. DESIGN-LOCKED decisions in this package are the planner's chosen implementation, authorized by the request for a complete plan. They are not claims that the author individually approved every detail. Treat them as the drafting contract until a deliberate revision is recorded.
4. The revised chapter map and rule tables override incompatible passages in the existing Chapter 1. Apply the specified opening patch before continuing.
5. The old manuscript is archaeological reference, not continuing canon. Do not import its events, terminology, or unresolved mechanics automatically.

### Author-locked requirements

- Original source: **Schlelberg - Draft 3 (1).docx**. The initially supplied **Schlelberg - BJB Draft.docx** was the wrong file and must not influence this project. No Hadni or Elara material may be imported from it.
- Manuscript in English; conversation in Indonesian.
- Selective poetic language and memorable sentences arising from lived events; no constant aphorisms.
- An inhabited Bronze Age village, inspired by Aegean/Mycenaean material culture, recently conquered: casualties, occupation, forced labor, confiscated harvest. This is an invented world, not historical Greece.
- A genuinely advanced future, where personal shields and specialized blades dominate the depicted close combat. Do not retrofit ordinary futuristic gunfights.
- Tobious's sword has legendary/heroic significance, comparable in narrative weight to a major mythic sword, without copying Excalibur's specific mythology.
- Preserve Tobious, Panjios, Suss, and the monsters. Their English species name is **the Iwang**, invariant plural; folk epithet **the Hollow Ones**. Singular: an Iwang / a Hollow One.
- Ypin is welcomed as a new character or replacement; this design makes him a core character.
- Earned training, setbacks, and a serious near-surrender arc.
- **Confirmed ending: Tobious and Mariya both survive, permanently separated into different eras.** No secret reopening, epilogue reunion, resurrection, alternate clone, or communication device that cancels the separation.
- Dates may change. The old 784 and 2039 are not mandatory.

## STORY CONTRACT

### Premise and reader promise

A farmer in an occupied Bronze Age village opens a buried threshold and sees an injured version of himself in a collapsing future city. Recruited because the threshold has bound itself to his nervous system, he learns to wield a legendary blade that can sever the connections between eras. When an attempted rescue traps Mariya and other villagers in an Iwang nest, he accepts a faster, more dangerous method from the commander training him. His mistake exposes the future to an incursion and costs his other mentor his life. He must then build a rescue with people he has treated as recipients of his decisions, stop a policy that counts the past as expendable, and close the only connection between their worlds. The successful closure leaves him in the future and Mariya in the life she chooses to rebuild in the past.

Promise: tactile occupied-village life, unsettling temporal wonder, beautiful advanced infrastructure with visible maintenance and inequality, learnable shield-and-blade combat, nonhuman monsters with coherent behavior, a romance between two deciding adults, a compromised mentor, and a victory achieved through collective judgment rather than greater damage output.

### What the novel argues through action

Question: does the ability to save people give you the right to choose what they lose?

Tobious's opening answer: if he becomes strong enough, he can protect people and prevent them from having to choose losses. His developed answer: power makes him responsible for what he does, not the owner of other people's lives. Good leadership includes disclosure, consent under pressure, delegation, and accepting that people may choose a life different from the one he wants for them.

Counterargument embodied by Suss: in an emergency, uncoordinated freedom kills; a leader must assign costs to others to secure the larger system. Suss is sometimes correct about urgency and coordination. He becomes the antagonist because he excludes the ancient population from moral and political standing, suppresses alternatives, and kills to preserve his authority over the calculation.

Panjios's partial answer: restraint matters, but shame and secrecy can make compassionate leadership paternalistic. His last action rectifies one immediate harm; it does not erase the people he abandoned twelve years earlier.

Mariya's answer: being loved is not consent to be removed from one's community. Her flaw is treating herself as an endlessly renewable resource. She must delegate and admit uncertainty rather than hold her group together through promises she cannot guarantee.

Ypin's answer: truth has practical value when it changes what happens to people. He must stop treating the preservation of proof as an automatic priority over rescue.

### What is preserved, rebuilt, removed, new

Preserved: farmer protagonist, his actual future voice, two eras, Mariya, Panjios, Suss, Iwang, dangerous power, mentor death, growth toward leadership, an epic blade, and a surviving group after conflict.

Rebuilt: training becomes timed practice plus operations; corruption becomes a knowingly accepted operating method and moral compromise, not an evil sword controlling every decision. Mariya acts inside captivity. Suss's betrayal has strategic purpose. Panjios's death follows Tobious's and Panjios's choices. The ending resolves this novel's threat.

Removed: repeated anonymous mentors, Aleron as an additional guide, the ungrounded 'you must sacrifice love' prophecy, random future trips, vague psychic tests, instant skill downloads, Toppan/Melissa turning into Rafiq/Lara, a new omniscient entity appearing in the ending, literal recruitment of everyone into a species called 'Toby' in the original.

New: occupation and grain administration, Aras/Veyr's shared terrain, Ypin's maintenance/data function, Edda as an independent clinician, finite portal sites, the Schlelberg overlap process, personhood classification fraud, a rigorously limited inception echo, and a planned evacuation that requires a permanent closure.

The formal organization is **the Concord**, its field operatives **Wardens**. Tobious remains a person's name. 'Leader' may appear as praise or recruitment rhetoric, not as a magic class or a title all characters inexplicably know. This is an intentional reconstruction decision: the original plural-Toby idea becomes shared wardenship, while the literal future self is preserved in the echo.

### Five movements and irreversible turns

#### I. Occupation and departure, Chapters 1–5

Tobious fails against a trained soldier while defending seed grain. Forced labor uncovers the threshold. The first opening is an image/sound echo; the subsequent live connection allows an Iwang through. Tobious helps shut it using worksite knowledge, injuring the local system and becoming its living reference. Panjios arrives because the monitoring system detects activation, not by narrative coincidence. A real extraction bargain supplies medicine and grain to Aras. Tobious goes to learn enough to return and protect people; he does not abandon Mariya without telling her.

First commitment: crossing to Veyr, accepting supervised calibration and training, and promising an honest return attempt rather than guaranteed rescue.

#### II. Instruction and evidence, Chapters 6–12

Future medicine helps but does not erase injuries. Suss demonstrates lifesaving competence. Tobious learns that survival in shield combat depends on timing and partners. The blade accepts his acquired field reference; publicity makes this look like a moral election. Ypin discovers that village populations have been counted as maintenance burden rather than protected people. Mariya independently collects labor tallies and tests the new supplies. Their observations converge before anyone announces a conspiracy.

Turn: Tobious joins a sanctioned return operation with evidence that its manifest conceals people. He now has reason to distrust orders, but not enough knowledge to treat all caution as cowardice.

#### III. Intervention and temptation, Chapters 13–20

Tobious prematurely severs a live transfer branch to release twelve workers. The choice succeeds locally and strands a second group, including Mariya, in the Receiver enclosure. Forty-eight civilians become captive anchors. Panjios orders withdrawal to prevent immediate expansion; Tobious obeys while hating the necessity. Mariya builds survival and escape arrangements. Ypin finds Panjios's older abandonment order and the current classification trick. Panjios admits his signature. Suss offers faster unguarded cuts and a seemingly workable selective rescue. Tobious accepts expanded operation after a successful, genuinely helpful trial. Captives then experience costs that the training display failed to represent.

Midpoint transformation: Tobious stops trying merely to qualify as a Warden and begins challenging the system; his first independent intervention also gives him personal culpability. Knowledge alone does not make his next choice wise.

#### IV. Failure and responsibility, Chapters 21–25

Suss uses the expanded connection to seize decisive control. Tobious initially follows the plan he accepted, then refuses the commanded sacrifice. Panjios holds an isolation shutter while Tobious and Ypin get people through. Suss kills Panjios to prevent him completing shutdown. Tobious escapes by Ypin's previously established maintenance route, not because Suss forgets to pursue. Official access is revoked; Suss publicly blames the incursion on Tobious's unauthorized behavior.

Tobious nearly surrenders his role. He considers handing back the sword and accepting Suss's isolation of the past. His recovery is several days of limited useful work, testimony from Mariya, verification of alternative calculations, and a chosen obligation—not one inspirational speech. Edda supports the alternative only after reviewing concrete casualty and infrastructure plans. Tobious discloses his errors to collaborators and lets them alter his plan.

Final commitment: evacuate the captives, prepare future services for disconnection, and permanently sever the root. Tobious learns before launch that the last operator cannot cross afterward. He does not conceal this fact from Mariya or Ypin.

#### V. Rescue and separation, Chapters 26–32

Mariya organizes the captive side of the escape. Tobious returns to Aras with a shield and Severance but resists the temptation to settle every occupation grievance by force. Two of the remaining forty-five prisoners die during the dangerous evacuation despite meaningful attempts to save them; forty-three reach safety. The rescue does not wait for Suss's defeat.

Tobious and Mariya explicitly consider crossing together. She chooses to remain in her own era, not because only a woman can perform care or because a new magical restriction forbids her crossing. Her home, work, and commitments are part of the life she wants. Tobious's task requires him to close the root from the future side. Their love is acknowledged and reciprocated; neither regards the other's decision as betrayal.

Suss attempts a one-sided purge that preserves Veyr's infrastructure while destroying the ancient node and its inhabited surroundings. Tobious, Ypin, and Edda's authorized emergency personnel disable this sequence. Suss is disarmed and taken alive. Tobious does not win a fair master-swordsman duel: he survives long enough to execute a team plan and gives up a killing opportunity to protect a rescue route.

In the final root overload, the inception echo connects Tobious to Chapter 1 for forty-one seconds. His shorter hair, left-eye scar, right-rib wound, speech, and the failing skyway match. He then completes the root cut. The mineral reference is destroyed; Severance's active core is spent. All three inter-era sites cease permanently. Shield technology and ordinary future infrastructure remain technically possible; rerouting and shortages continue.

Tobious survives with a lasting right-hand deficit. Ypin stays as a friend and collaborator, not a replacement romantic partner. Evidence sufficient for an inquiry survives even though Ypin abandoned the most comprehensive cache during rescue. Panjios is remembered with his culpability intact. Mariya's final chapter shows surviving Aras households sowing the seed they preserved, negotiating practical autonomy from a weakened garrison, and refusing to substitute a hero cult for communal decisions. The book closes its rescue, monster breach, institutional exposure, romance, and identity promises. No last-page larger villain.

### Why Tobious must remain in the future

The root's material control seam is physically in Veyr. Severance must be in contact and receive continuous living feedback from the bound reference for the final cut. There is no remote or timed activation. A replacement reference requires seven uninterrupted days with every inter-era branch idle; the captives' anchor chamber cannot be idled without killing them and releasing the nest. After their release, convergence leaves hours, not a week. The existence of this transfer method and its conditions is established early, not revealed during the goodbye.

Once the root is cut, all local receiver routes fail together. Closing one gate while leaving another is not a solution: the endangered volume is one lattice with three access sites. The material sustaining the chronological offset is consumed; no spare root exists for these eras. There is no independent portal to the same past elsewhere.

Mariya could elect to settle in Veyr before the closure. She chooses not to. Do not write a false physical prohibition merely to force the agreed ending. The machinery limits Tobious; the relationship respects Mariya's agency.

### Main causal chain

Telassar occupies a productive harbor → future-linked prospectors exploit the occupation to excavate a buried receiver → forced labor puts Tobious at its damaged contact → he becomes the reference and sees the echo → reopening draws an Iwang → damage forces intervention by Wardens → Panjios needs Tobious's consent and skill development → Tobious sees both future competence and exclusion → evidence from Mariya and Ypin prompts intervention → premature severance saves one group but strands another → urgency and distrust of Panjios make Suss's shortcut attractive → amplified operation enables a breach and Suss's seizure of control → Tobious's refusal forces a retreat Panjios holds open at fatal cost → grief and guilt almost end Tobious's participation → verified alternatives and other people's choices restore limited responsibility → a joint rescue removes the captives before the root decision → Suss loses leverage and attempts a purge → team action stops him → final severance fulfills the future-self echo and separates the eras → survivors must build lives without undoing the cost.

## WORLD BIBLE AND NON-NEGOTIABLE OPERATING RULES

### 1. Time and the two societies

Planning dates: ancient **Year 718**, future **Year 3318**, in a fictional continuous coastal chronology. Exactly 2,600 years separate paired sites. Do not identify either with Gregorian years or real Greek history. The opening need not print a date. Establish the elapsed centuries in Chapter 6 through a comprehensible demonstration, not a lecture or Tobious instantly reading unfamiliar digits.

One history exists. The inhabitants cannot overwrite established events, create replacement selves, or reverse deaths. Regular gates pair an ancient date with the corresponding future date 2,600 years later. A day lived on one side is a day elapsed at the paired endpoint. There are no training-time discounts, movable destination dates, or arbitrary stopovers in other centuries.

Future historical records are fragmentary. False manifests and selective classifications, not a mutable universe, explain the apparent contradiction between recorded 'no protected inhabitants' and actual captives. Actions in Aras are part of the history that produced Veyr. Do not show Veyr changing retroactively when someone moves a stone or decides differently in Aras.

The locals' years and the future's dates are reconciled by Concord archaeology; ancient peasants generally count harvests and reigns. Both names for years belong in records, not constant narration.

### 2. Geography: one landscape seen across centuries

**Aras:** approximately eighty households on terraces overlooking a narrow bay. Mud-brick upper walls on stone feet; shared granary; threshing floor; olive groves above, barley and pulses below; boatbuilding yard at the shore; narrow lanes; a communal well and ancestor terrace over much older masonry. No marble metropolis or uniform fantasy tavern district. Houses show maintenance, repaired baskets, soot, stored water, and people still trying to work after invasion.

**Telassari citadel:** ridge across the valley, ninety minutes' ordinary walking from Aras; occupied former local palace. It commands the road and harbor levy. Telassar is a native expansionist palace kingdom, not a disguised future civilization. Its ruler wants grain, labor, and access to the bay. The conquest is real politics; not every soldier knows anything about temporal technology.

**Old Storehouse / Receiver:** under the ancestor terrace; ordinary upper granary is a different building. Entry through stairs, a passage, a circular flooded chamber, then lower steps and a black threshold. Preserve these relative positions. Old drains run toward the bay. The central receiver and connected annex become the captive enclosure after Chapter 13. The inhabitants are phase-tethered but still located on the ancient side; they are not in a time-stopped pocket universe.

**Salt Gate:** auxiliary receiver in abandoned salt storage near the shore, forty minutes downhill from Aras, twenty minutes farther along the coast to its concealed mouth. Used for small scheduled crossings and supply. Do not invent an ocean voyage for this walk.

**Quarry Gate:** emergency auxiliary in inland stone workings, three hours uphill from Aras, connected to the same root. Useful for field access, not a loophole after closure. Closed by damage during the failed operation; parts of its service channel remain usable for information and planned evacuation.

**Veyr:** the future city occupying the valley and cliffs above the old bay. Shoreline engineering and centuries of rebuilding obscure ancient alignments. The citadel ridge is now the foundation of the Civic Spine. Layered walkways span open air; transit runs in guided channels; sun-control surfaces shade housing; food grows in controlled towers and reservoirs support civic cooling. These systems have operators and repair access. Wealthy districts experience them as effortless; service districts live beside heat, noise, and ration interruptions.

**Root Hall:** original field root beneath the Civic Spine, containing the physical seam Severance must cut. The Wardens' training rooms, memorial gallery, and Concord control offices are nearby but distinct. Access is controlled by credentials and human guards, not the sword acting as a universal door key.

**Lower Service Ward:** Ypin's work district, Edda's clinic, manual reservoirs, retired maintenance walks. It becomes a place of refuge because Ypin knows its people and routes before the crisis. It is not magically invisible to authorities; fugitives move cautiously, receive limited help, and face searches.

**High Concourse:** transit/structural junction above Root Hall. The skyway and skyline seen in Chapter 1 appear here in Chapter 31. Write the later view from the reverse position with the same road segment and light conditions.

### 3. Ancient life, economy, and occupation

Bronze Age Aegean inspiration: barley, legumes, figs, olives, wool, linen, pottery, bronze blades/tools, stone weights, seals, clay administrative records, pack animals, palatial storage, and large older foundations. Grain and labor obligations dominate; do not introduce coin wages casually. Common farm tools include wood and stone as well as scarce bronze fittings. Soldiers' equipment differs by rank. There are no classical Spartan institutions, Roman legions, medieval steel plate, or generic ancient democracy.

The king's altered measure makes domination concrete. Twelve of fourteen seed jars are seized in Chapter 1, two officially reserved. They are a specific shared seed reserve, not the entirety of eighty households' food. Other household stores and garden crops exist but are being depleted. One remaining jar will be distributed during acute hunger; one is preserved for the final sowing. Record this, rather than producing unlimited hidden grain.

Occupation includes forced repair, detained laborers, controlled exits, transport levies, and confiscated sacred property. It also includes native cooperation, bargaining, soldiers who follow ordinary incentives, and disagreements about resistance. Do not make every Telassari a sadist or every villager united and brave. The officer's insistence on a usable laborer explains why Tobious is punished with work rather than executed in Chapter 1.

Aras has customary household meetings and shared storage, not universal modern parliamentary institutions. Mariya's final authority comes from recognizable work and surviving people's consent. The political ending wins a seasonal relief/withdrawal arrangement and practical local control, not the magical disappearance of Telassar.

The woman-with-bowl statue and first-water offering are invented local practice. Removal wounds communal continuity. No actual goddess needs to manifest or reveal the sword. Religion offers rituals of grief and obligation but does not supply a last-minute solution.

Language: the prose translates both societies into readable English. Panjios has learned the ancient regional language through field work. Future interpretation devices help Tobious understand routine speech but do not instantly teach him technical literacy or make him literate in old palace script. Ypin occasionally has to restate a technical phrase; do not make this a recurring joke at Tobious's expense.

### 4. Future society and institutions

The Concord maintains the inter-era lattice and trains Wardens. It is an institution with public responsibilities, competing departments, and real competence. It is not a uniformly evil cult. 'Truth' is part of its legitimating rhetoric: verified records are treated as a basis for authority, which allows classification fraud to matter.

Suss commands field operations and can invoke emergency access. Panjios controls training/calibration and has old authority but cannot freely overrule every operational order. Ypin is a maintenance and manifest technician with bounded permissions; Edda is an independent clinic physician with civil-emergency authority and patients whose testimony matters.

A technical resource label, **unregistered biological load**, is used for ancient people outside protected-person counts. A survey can truthfully report zero protected residents while concealing dozens of humans. The distinction must be made legible with two actual columns and a name the reader knows. Do not hide behind pages of jargon.

Medicine: disinfecting, imaging, wound sealing, splinting, and assisted rehabilitation are advanced. Nerves, blood loss, deep structural damage, and infection still impose recovery. No resurrection, instant regeneration, or downloading combat skills. Tobious can remain sore, angry, dependent, and impatient without portraying care as weakness.

Communication/transit: reliable within ordinary Veyr coverage, interrupted by thick service masonry and active lattice distortion. Fixed paired service relays can transmit narrow-band audio/text during powered diagnostic windows. There is no live omniscient view of captives. Survey wafers can record and buffer messages. They cannot open gates, locate everyone, or supply unlimited power. Specific relay openings are logged in the timeline.

Military logic: this novel depicts fighting around vulnerable urban infrastructure, confined receivers, and captives. Precision capture and preservation matter. Heavy demolition exists and is dangerous; shields do not make bombardment physically impossible. Its use around the root risks Schlelberg and is an unacceptable substitute for rescue. Do not claim that all warfare everywhere has become honorable dueling.

### 5. The lattice and Schlelberg

The paired system consists of one root in Veyr and three receiver accesses in the ancient volume: Storehouse, Salt, Quarry. All share one irreplaceable mineral phase-reference. The root is a relic of an earlier technically capable culture, usually called the First Makers; present engineers can service interfaces but cannot recreate the phase-reference. Ancient builders later enclosed surviving structures in the masonry under Aras. This history is background, not a third active era or an unanswered new cosmic villain.

Schlelberg is the name for **runaway overlap between the paired inhabited volumes**. The damaged lattice begins stabilizing its load by drawing on living nervous systems; Iwang exploit the gradients and establish nests. Increasing overlap produces displaced surfaces, shared pressure effects, failed doors, and future service surges. Do not describe it as a mood, an unseen dark king, or a virus with whatever effects a scene needs.

The measured convergence deadline is Day 84 after Chapter 1. Early estimates are uncertain; by Chapter 17, independent observations agree on the operating window. This is an engineering deadline caused by accumulated stress and predictable load, not an arbitrary celestial prophecy. Suss's unauthorized gain shortens local safety margins but does not move the final deadline differently in each chapter.

There are three strategies: (a) continue using captive anchors, extending suffering and losing control; (b) Suss's terminal purge destroys the ancient receiver volume while protecting the future root and its useful infrastructure; (c) evacuate first, prepare future services to disconnect, then destroy the root reference and all temporal access permanently. Strategy (c) is difficult, not a painless clever third door. People die during rescue; Veyr suffers damage, rationing, and displacement.

Why not evacuate Aras earlier? Before Chapter 13, general resettlement is refused by Concord policy and native occupation control, not falsely presented as a universal law of physics. Panjios can negotiate supplies and exceptional patients, proving the restriction is political. After captivity begins, people are tethered inside a guarded receiver enclosure; removal requires sequential release and safe routes. This change must be explicit.

### 6. The inception echo: the only backward information exception

The first contact at the damaged Storehouse interface acquires Tobious as the living reference. Root closure on Day 84 generates a single **forty-one-second sensory echo** back to that reference's initial acquisition. It is not a selectable communications service. Images and sound pass; bodies, air, weapons, and heat do not. Chapter 2 establishes this with a physical test. The water going still and the lamp flame deforming in Chapter 1 are local field effects, not wind from the future.

After the echo ends, the still-powered receiver can open an ordinary physical route to the paired current future date. The first monster enters through this second state, not through the echo. Distinguish their visible behavior in Chapter 2.

There is no later replay from the past and no continuous prediction. The inception words were always those spoken at closure; Tobious cannot edit them into a different history. Future logs show a diagnostic anomaly at Day 0 but not a complete recording of the Day-84 conversation. Nobody has an omniscient film of the plot. Explain enough by Chapters 12 and 24 that the final recognition is earned.

The existing opening's final accusation is revised: 'They aren't looking for stores. That door is what they came for. And now they know you can open it.' This preserves menace without implying Suss or the army knew Tobious by name before his acquisition. The echo man is Tobious after twelve weeks of injury and change, not decades older. His apparent age is an impression, consistent with Chapter 1.

### 7. Living reference and why the sword accepts Tobious

The interface's normal synthetic regulator is broken. During direct conductive contact, it acquires the first viable nervous system as a temporary reference. Blood is incidental conductive contact; no hereditary blood test or chosen royal lineage. The overseer did not know which worker would be acquired. Direct contact makes Tobious locally compatible, not globally strongest or morally chosen.

Transferring the reference is possible only after seven uninterrupted days of idle receivers, with old and new references present for supervised calibration. Establish this in Chapter 9 through procedure, and revisit it when Tobious asks to withdraw. The tethered prisoners prevent an idle week during captivity. After evacuation, the terminal window is too short. No volunteer swap at the last minute.

A reference is necessary for active severance but does not give telepathy, foresight, universal healing, command over monsters, or automatic fighting skill. His body experiences accumulated strain. Medical personnel can measure it; ideology may interpret it as destiny. The narrative must keep those apart.

### 8. Shields and combat

Personal shields spread and redirect sudden incoming impulse. Ordinary thrown/fired projectiles and simple energy strikes are inefficient against intact units. A powered blade uses sustained near-contact feedback to couple through one small sector. Detached projectiles cannot maintain this operator-controlled coupling. Exotic demolition tools can attack shields, but are not portable miracle pistols introduced to invalidate established combat.

Canonical rhythm: acquire blade contact/near-contact for roughly three heartbeats; engage penetration; the attacker's own full shield drops during the coupled strike; recover and restore the shield. Training teaches distance, feints, control of the opposing blade, protection by partners, and retreat. Highly skilled fighters can shorten the acquisition through preparation, not ignore it entirely. A novice cannot win against Suss by becoming angry.

Shields distribute momentum but do not remove gravity, bad footing, suffocation, falls, exhaustion, or every crushing load. A shove can move the wearer. Units have heat and charge limits. A quarter-hour of light use is not the same as repeated heavy impacts; show warning states rather than arbitrary percentages. An ordinary unit can be serviced from standard city power; temporal root closure does not abolish every shield in Veyr.

Ancient soldiers have bronze weapons and hide shields, not secret personal fields. A future-shielded Tobious has a major advantage against them, which creates an ethical temptation; he still cannot stand indefinitely against a crowd or rescue people by recklessly triggering feedback near the root.

### 9. Severance, the Unbroken Edge

Official proper name **Severance**; old ceremonial epithet **the Unbroken Edge**. Physical object: a restrained, pale metal blade with a narrow dark channel, a plain grip repaired by earlier hands, and a small opaque core in the pommel. No floating interface, endless colored beams, cartoon enlargement, or sword speaking as an extra mentor. Preserve its recognizable silhouette.

It is the last serviceable root-cutting key from the First Makers, celebrated because an earlier bearer ended an incursion at personal cost. Later propaganda turns access into legitimacy. The legend contains a real deed but not proof that every bearer is good. Its rarity follows the irreplaceable core material, not convenient refusal to manufacture ordinary swords.

Modes: ordinary blade (no superhuman skill); guarded field cut (local connection only, approximately three heartbeats of coupling, shield down, then at least thirty seconds for the grip/core to settle); unguarded cut (Suss's bypass suppresses the recovery interlock, can repeat more quickly but stores load in the carrier and connected receiver bodies); terminal root severance (continuous contact and living feedback for fifteen heartbeats; consumes the reference and active core permanently).

The guarded mode cannot free an entire nest with one swing. Prisoner tethers must be isolated sequentially and the exit route kept stable. The bypass genuinely saves time in controlled tests. The lie is the incomplete accounting of who absorbs its cost in a live network, not that it never works.

The sword does not steal souls, judge virtue, fix injuries, open unrelated locks, cut any concept, or grant a new mode in the finale. At the end the inert physical blade remains broken/spent. It can be preserved as evidence, never restored into a back door home.

### 10. The Iwang / the Hollow Ones

Nonhuman organisms adapted to the interstitial habitat around phase gradients. They were never secretly villagers or future Tobious clones. Individuality exists through behavior, not humanoid villain speeches. The nickname comes from visible open spaces in their torsos and the sound they produce when air passes through those cavities; it does not assert the absence of souls.

Baseline form: tall, articulated bodies, hard pale external struts, dark tissues around a collar/throat nexus, flexible gripping limbs. Their silhouette is recognizable. They can flatten through an already thinned boundary near a live gradient, not walk through any intact wall at will. Attack requires local coherence, exposing the collar nexus. This provides a learnable but dangerous opening for physical weapons.

Signals: mineral clicking, a pressure change across a nearby shield, dust/water responding to a gradient before the body appears. Use a limited repeatable vocabulary. Do not give each appearance a new sensory power. They do not mimic loved ones' voices or read memories in this version.

They preserve captive humans as living stabilizers and take field energy, not ordinary meals from them. Humans still need water, food, sanitation, and movement. Darran's handlers supply minimal rations under Suss's concealment scheme to keep the anchors alive; the monsters are not inexplicably feeding people. Mariya's organization and captured stores matter when that supply falters.

Suss directs routes with energized baffles and can temporarily keep a work corridor unattractive to the Iwang. He cannot command their minds. Removing baffles can expose his own people. Monsters can die, withdraw, adapt to repeated feints, and be driven back by root closure. The ending ends their access to these inhabited volumes, not the species throughout existence.

### 11. Daily life that makes the worlds worth saving

Aras has communal labor without perfect communal harmony. Households contest irrigation turns, borrow animals, remember debts in goods and labor, and argue over whose roof should be repaired first. A child's coming-of-age is marked by a first full share of harvest work, not a universal warrior initiation. Marriage and households can be negotiated through kin and property, but Tobious and Mariya are unmarried adults able to choose their relationship. Avoid casually imposing a specific historical Greek law on this invented society.

Education is practical and unequal: children learn tasks, songs, local routes, and household measures; administrative literacy belongs largely to palace workers and some storekeepers. Mariya's limited tally literacy gives her leverage without making her a modern accountant in costume. Tobious can recognize his household mark and ordinary quantities without reading every document. Occupation law is enforced through collective levies, detention, household liability, and access to land. Customary negotiation survives beneath imposed authority.

Future education provides broad basic literacy and highly specialized training. Most citizens can use infrastructure they cannot rebuild. This gap matters: Ypin understands service interfaces but not lost reference synthesis. Public transit and routine care are normal expectations; registration determines access to some protections and emergency priority. News travels through civic displays and personal devices, with command notices identified by issuer. Suss can publish an official emergency claim quickly; he cannot delete every independent witness or media channel. Edda's eventual case works by authenticated evidence, corroboration, and civic authority, not one viral post that instantly ends all resistance.

Food in Veyr includes cultivated greens, grain grown in controlled beds, fermented protein, and household preparation. Imported luxuries exist but do not need a trade atlas. Leisure includes shared cooking, open-air viewing terraces, music transmitted through public sound surfaces at agreed hours, and neighborhood repair games. Ypin enjoys taking apart harmless objects and is occasionally bad at putting a domestic one back together; Tobious enjoys making food with his hands and recognizes weather that the city tries to regulate. Their friendship needs a pleasure the plot could momentarily leave alone.

Future wealth is visible as quiet, space, reliable shade, and distance from service heat; poverty is interruption, crowding, and having to negotiate a repair. This is more specific than rich people wearing black and poor people wearing gray. Neither era is morally pure. Advanced medicine is a genuine good; ancient communities can be coercive or exclusionary. Choosing Aras is Mariya's choice of a life, not the novel proving modernity inherently false.

### 13. Epic scale without constant catastrophe

Use six distinct visual peaks: the occupied grain court; the impossible city beyond cold ankle-deep water; Tobious's first full view of Veyr above the changed bay; Severance's restrained first live coupling against a dark barrier; a line of people moving beneath the pale Iwang through the old masonry; and the failing skyway viewed from the other end of the opening image. Each has a human objective and a cost. Between them, let meals, work, disagreement, desire, and landscape restore scale.

The sword's heroic weight comes from history, public expectation, recognizable appearance, and the bearer having to remain exposed while others cover him. Give its gallery scene ceremonial space and silence, but do not reveal an ancient monarchy or copy a sword-in-stone test. Shield combat emphasizes close contact, coordinated vulnerability, and infrastructure constraints. Avoid importing Dune's specific mythology, terminology, factions, or iconic combat explanation verbatim.

Schlelberg grows through three visible stages: early local boundary anomalies (Ch1–12); linked captivity and service surges (Ch13–23); city-scale structural overlap and threatened ancient-volume purge (Ch24–31). Keep the Iwang frightening at each scale through learned limitations being hard to exploit under pressure, not new arbitrary powers.

### 14. Operational clarifications for the final rescue

After the Chapter-57 overload, a forecast low-load trough on Days 82–83 is the earliest safe opportunity for mass untethering. Seed the changing load pattern in Chapter 17; calculate the usable trough with Mariya's observed baffle intervals in Chapter 24. The buildup culminates on Day 84. Thus preparation time is necessary, and only hours remain for root closure after evacuation. Selective extraction earlier is possible at redistributed cost, explaining Suss's offer without permitting an effortless general rescue. No new cosmic alignment governs the dates.

The freed Iwang do not become unlimited animals loose forever in ordinary space. Outside an active gradient they lose coherence within minutes and retreat or die; a coherent strike can still kill before that. Root closure removes their sustainable access throughout the paired volumes. This limitation should be observed in Chapter 2's retreat and taught in Chapter 6, then relied on in Chapter 31. Killing Suss alone does not close a gradient.

Edda is both a clinician and a registered civil casualty-response convener. Her independent emergency powers are introduced through routine work in Chapter 6 and stated in Chapter 15, before they affect the climax. She can convene review, request marshals, preserve medical records, and protect service access under an issued order; she cannot convict, command all Wardens, or operate the root. Suss contests that order using field authority and loyal staff, producing a split command rather than instant universal compliance.

## CHARACTER AND RELATIONSHIP BIBLE

All ages, motives, and backstories here are design decisions for the reconstruction, not facts recovered from the old draft. Keep five central characters. Supporting characters have bounded jobs; do not expand each into another secret lineage.

Naming revision 1.1: use full names in narration, chapter cards, records, and official address. Familiar names are Tobi (Tobious), Panji (Panjios), and Lio (Lios); introduce each connection naturally. See `naming-canon.md` for all 18 confirmed names and pronunciation guides.

### Tobious — 23 at Chapter 1

Aras farmer and sluice repairer; physically capable, practically observant, initially poor at literate administration and entirely untrained against organized fighters. Weather-darkened skin, working hands, dark hair long enough to tie; appearance is ordinary within his region. His mother's leaf mark on a jar is a family object, not a hereditary crest. His parents died of an ordinary fever three years before the conquest; introduce briefly only when context needs it. Do not add a murdered sibling or royal parent. Samon is an older neighbor, not a secret father.

Want: restore safety and agency to his home, then recover Mariya and the others. Need: distinguish responsibility from total control and accept the judgments of people he loves. Wound: fleeing the fallen gate and later failing to protect seed have made strength feel equivalent to moral worth. False belief: with enough power he can prevent anyone important to him from having to lose. Strength: persistence, observation of flows and dependencies, willingness to work, ability to remember individuals rather than categories. Flaw: treats refusal or delay as betrayal when frightened; commits before checking how a system distributes costs.

Contradiction: he resents decisions made over his head, then makes decisions for Mariya. Secret: initially hides the extent of his attraction to Suss's bypass and the residual warning signs. Boundary: he will not deliberately use an innocent person as expendable bait; his arc tests whether he notices when his 'necessary' action does effectively that.

Arc decisions: resist seed seizure (fails); accept a stated bargain with Panjios (conditional trust); choose training while impatient (earned progress); cut the transfer early (saves twelve, strands forty-eight); accept bypass after a useful success (culpability); refuse captive sacrifice during the breach (costly reversal); almost relinquish role (low point); disclose the risk and accept others' revisions (leadership); honor Mariya's decision; prioritize the route over killing Suss; complete severance knowing separation is permanent.

End: alive in Veyr, with a persistent right-hand tremor/weak grip following root overload. He can work and learn adaptations; do not render him useless or spiritually perfected. He has no automatic command throne. He assists recovery with Ypin and participates in public testimony.

Voice: concrete, initially terse when ashamed; farming/repair comparisons only where he would actually make them. Vocabulary expands through instruction without becoming engineering fluency overnight. His close narration can be lyrical, but he does not speak in continuous maxims. Anger often precedes embarrassment; humor emerges around ordinary work.

### Mariya — 24

Aras resident, longtime companion and developing lover of Tobious. Helps manage shared stores and mill work. Her competence comes from years of measuring grain, tracking household obligations, and coordinating people. She reads familiar quantities and names on administrative tallies but is not fluent in every palace document. Dark hair, burned shawl trimmed unevenly, damaged thumb skin; later fatigue and specific clothing repairs persist.

Want: keep her community alive and preserve its ability to choose how it lives. Need: let others share responsibility and tell the truth about uncertainty instead of promising more than she can deliver. Wound: as stores assistant she once obeyed an allocation that excluded an unlisted widow's household; quiet remembered failure, not a new tragedy subplot. False belief: if she stops holding everything together, everyone else will collapse. Strength: negotiation, memory for people and quantities, practical triage, ability to earn cooperation. Flaw: overwork, impatience with flight, manipulates hope when afraid a group will fragment.

Contradiction: resents Tobious deciding for her but initially assigns tasks to other captives without asking. Secret: spends part of the protected seed reserve during hunger before telling the households whose future crop it represents. She later discloses the decision and accepts objections. Boundary: does not buy her own freedom by selecting an unwilling person to replace her.

Arc: negotiate two jars; organize supply and information while Tobious is away; test rather than worship the future promise; stay to guide workers during the failed intervention; build captive cooperation; a failed escape and deaths expose the danger of false certainty; disclose what she does and does not know; delegate essential evacuation tasks; survive through combined actions; choose her own era openly, without declaring Tobious selfish for needing to leave.

Romance: they know each other's working habits and share pre-existing affection, but neither starts with an unexamined marriage claim. Chapter 5 admits they want a future together. Chapter 13 reunion includes affection and a concrete disagreement. Chapter 18 communication revisits an ordinary shared memory and establishes that saving her alone is not her wish. Chapter 29 is a mutual, informed goodbye. No jealousy triangle. No sexual violence as shorthand for occupation or captivity. If intimacy is included, keep it brief, consensual, and emotional rather than explicit; no scene is required merely to establish adulthood.

End: living in Aras, co-organizing reconstruction rather than ruling alone. She grieves an inaccessible living person and still makes plans. Do not frame her as a widow waiting for a portal to reopen or imply she must remain romantically frozen forever.

Voice: direct, specific, capable of dry correction. Under strain she asks for counts, time, and names. She can be unfair when exhausted; other captives may successfully challenge her. Her POV includes bodies, storage, tasks, competing needs, and beauty unrelated to Tobious.

### Ypin — 22

Veyr maintenance and route-record technician. Slim, quick with his hands, cropped hair, repaired work jacket, a practical tool roll; ordinary social standing. No hidden nobility, prodigy who understands everything, or comic accent. He knows his system because he services it.

Want: expose the discrepancy between transport records and living people, and keep service-ward residents from paying for decisions made above them. Need: make truth serve people rather than his desire to be vindicated. Wound: has spent eighteen months signing manifests he believed described inert loads; discovers that his work helped conceal human transfers. This is complicity, not an extra murdered-relative revenge story. False belief: once the complete evidence is secured, justice will follow automatically. Strength: precise observation, repair, permissions knowledge, willingness to question rank. Flaw: hoards information until proof is perfect and can prioritize a recorder over an endangered person.

Relationship with Tobious: mutual irritation becomes earned partnership. He resents the resources allocated to one outsider while his ward struggles; Tobious resents being treated as a calibration problem. Shared maintenance work lets each see the other's competence. No romantic competition over Mariya.

Decisions: help Tobious understand care instead of mocking him; preserve contradictory logs; confront Tobious's selective concern; risk credentials on a verified rescue; abandon the most complete archive to get living people out in Chapter 21; rebuild a sufficient evidence chain with Edda; openly accept the danger of covering Tobious's shield-down cuts; stay after victory as a friend, not a follower awarded to the hero.

Limits: cannot unlock every door, manufacture a new root, learn lost Maker physics overnight, or replace a reference without the stated calibration. His bypasses exploit access he already has or hardware shown earlier. He suffers a left-forearm burn in Chapter 21; fine work remains slower for the rest of the book.

Voice: exact, skeptical, often funny about concrete inconvenience. Jokes are not constant tension-breaking. In grief he becomes unusually spare. Avoid making him an internet-savvy contemporary dropped into fantasy speech.

### Panjios — 58

Senior Warden instructor; field experience with ancient communities. Calm because he prepares, not because the narrative labels him wise. Gray hair, an old stiffness in the left knee, plain training clothes. Speaks the ancient regional language and can enter Aras under an existing technical-services arrangement with the occupation authorities.

Want: contain Schlelberg and prevent Tobious repeating earlier failures. Need: surrender the protection afforded by secrecy and let others judge the information. Wound: twelve paired years earlier, he signed closure of a route during an incursion, abandoning ninety-seven people in the ancient zone. He believed it would prevent a larger loss, then permitted records to remove their personhood. False belief: telling novices everything before they are ready will cause another catastrophe. Strength: listening, teaching in increments, patience under pressure, tactical restraint. Flaw: paternalism and delayed disclosure.

Contradiction: teaches Tobious to respect other people's choices while withholding the information necessary for choice. Secret: his signature, his knowledge of the accounting convention, and his failure to confront Suss earlier. Boundary: he will not knowingly feed current captives into a planned stabilization once an actual alternative is available; his previous failure makes this decision costly, not effortless.

No magical cleansing blast, transferred soul, or strength download.

Voice: practical questions, particular remembered mistakes, some warmth. He sometimes answers too late. A useful line must answer a present problem, not sound like a detached inspirational quotation.

### Suss — 46

Concord field commander and Tobious's combat instructor. Fit, economical, observant. Initially saves people in view of Tobious, remembers equipment limits, and improves a student's survival. No early sneering, villain costume, repeated 'dark eyes,' or narrator declarations that he is evil.

Want: preserve Veyr's stability and his authority to determine acceptable losses; retain the root as infrastructure and strategic power. Need he refuses: recognize ancient inhabitants as equal persons and submit his decisions to informed scrutiny. Wound: an earlier delayed evacuation in Veyr killed people while administrators argued; he learned the wrong general lesson that disagreement itself is the enemy. Strength: genuine tactical judgment, bravery, clear instruction, operational planning. Flaw: selective moral accounting, refusal to relinquish control, suppression of inconvenient evidence. Contradiction: keeps promises to individuals while designing systems that exploit classes of people. Secret: backing the prospecting arrangement, using detainees as stabilizers, and suppressing feasible disconnection planning.

He kills Panjios only when the latter is physically preventing the seizure of control, making the act instrumental rather than a random reveal.

End: disarmed alive, detained by civil emergency personnel supported by Edda's evidence. No 'I will return' stinger. His policy faces public examination; a full courtroom sequel is not required.

### Style and tone

### Hard plot-hole tests and answers

**Why Tobious instead of a trained operator?** He is the first viable nervous system acquired by a broken regulator. Others could have served. Before captivity, transfer is possible but costly and slow; he explicitly chooses training and the return opportunity. Panjios favors teaching the current reference, and Suss favors a recruit with urgent personal stakes. The text must admit this was a decision, not impossible biology disguised as destiny.

**Why doesn't Suss kill Tobious or simply force his hand?** Severance needs conscious adaptive coupling. A dead carrier or a mechanically moved hand cannot supply it. Trust, an apparently viable rescue, and urgent personal motives are useful to Suss. After rejection he tries to seize control and use the separate destructive purge, which preserves his future infrastructure at ancient lives' expense. He has a fallback, not infinite control of Tobious.

**Why train for weeks while people suffer?** Basic reference use and shield survival require time; the future does not initially authorize general resettlement. The source's unresolved hostage gap is removed by placing captivity after the first six weeks of training. Mariya monitors real worsening labor conditions meanwhile. Once forty-eight are tethered, training and operations become morally urgent, not leisurely side quests.

**Why cannot another person take over during the forty-two days after capture?** Transfer requires every receiver idle for seven consecutive days. Idling tethered captives kills them. After the Day-57 breach, the first safe mass-release trough is Days 82–83; Day 84 remains the terminal limit. State both rules before the final operation. Treat this as a demanding fictional engineering model, not established physics.

**Why wait for Day 82?** The preceding operation leaves load oscillations too strong for mass untethering. Mariya's measurements and independent field data identify a finite low-load trough. The intervening days prepare routes, transport, care, civic protection, and alternate future utilities. Selective extraction is possible earlier only by transferring its load to those left behind; that is Suss's tempting offer. Avoid a late, unexplained 'we must wait for the moon' restriction.

**Why can't they close from the past, throw the sword through, or use a timer?** The material seam is at the future root. Terminal severance requires fifteen heartbeats of the reference's conscious corrective feedback in physical contact. Throwing or an unattended mechanism loses coupling. Remote operation and unsupported new automation cannot be introduced at the last minute.

**Why not use Quarry Gate afterward or build another gate?** Quarry, Salt, and Storehouse are accesses to one root reference, not independent time machines. Final severance consumes the irreplaceable reference material and active blade core. Engineers can maintain interfaces, not synthesize a replacement chronological offset. There is no extant separate route to this ancient era. Do not tease one in the aftermath.

**Why not simply evacuate everyone to the future?** Physically possible before closure; politically and practically difficult but not universally forbidden. The initial block is policy and occupation, later the tethers. The final operation prioritizes safe release to ancient homes, with relocation an individual option. Mariya is expressly offered it and declines for a life she wants. Her choice is emotionally debatable; the narrative must respect that rather than prove her objectively correct.

**Why doesn't Mariya ask Tobious to abandon the task for her?** She knows this would expose their communities to overlap or purge. She also knows she could cross. Their honest alternatives make the ending a painful choice between real lives, not a five-minute misunderstanding. Nerissa and others can carry on without her; she is not held hostage by the claim that only she can keep the village alive.

**Why are the captives alive for weeks?** Living nervous systems stabilize the field. Darran's human handlers ration food, water, and sanitation space under the concession; Iwang exploit gradients and do not cook for prisoners. Accumulated strain and inadequate care kill three before evacuation. Rescue logistics reflect weakened bodies rather than treating every prisoner as a fresh runner.

**Why not bomb or shoot the enemy?** Personal shields frustrate ordinary projectiles; blades use close feedback, with an exposed attacking interval. Heavy demolition remains possible but would destroy captives and the critical root, worsening the disaster. No gunfight is required in the book. Do not overstate the premise as proof that every weapon in the universe has become a sword.

**Why doesn't future medicine erase the ending's costs?** Treatment improves survival; nerve damage, blood loss, and accumulated structural injury still require time or remain lasting. Skills cannot be downloaded. Edda's limits are established before losses, not invoked only when the plot wants tragedy.

**Why doesn't Edda arrest Suss in Chapter 15?** A physician's suspicion is not a criminal verdict or control of the Wardens. Her registered civic role can convene review with sufficient evidence and an actionable emergency plan. Later, command splits and restricted access require marshals to reach him. The Chapter-30 detention is a constrained operation, not an unexplained badge that solves the plot.

**Why do monsters stop when the root closes?** Their coherence depends on active gradients. Closure removes sustained access; those briefly stranded lose coherence or retreat. This is observed early. The species still exists in its habitat, but this book's invasion route is genuinely ended.

**Why doesn't one timeline make all decisions meaningless?** The book does not present branching counterfactual histories to undo consequences. Characters still deliberate from incomplete knowledge; their choices are what constitute the history. The echo contains forty-one seconds, not proof that everybody lives. Avoid speeches claiming that fate absolves Tobious or that he had no responsibility because an image existed.

### Risk register: what a good outline cannot guarantee

1. **Administrative monotony.** Names and records carry the theme but must not become identical scenes of finding a document. The cards alternate bodily risk, work, intimacy, wonder, and decisions. If a record scene only repeats the last one, combine or dramatize it through resistance and consequence.
2. **An engineered ending.** The transfer rule, deadline, low-load trough, and Mariya's choice could feel manufactured if introduced together late. Seed in Chapters 9, 17, 18, and 24. Give Mariya a credible wish to live at home, not only duties. Let readers mourn the alternative instead of dismissing it.
3. **Too little mythic feeling.** A technical sword can become a maintenance tool in presentation. Preserve ceremony, history, public longing, material beauty, and the astonishing scale of the task. Its mechanics constrain the legend; they do not replace emotional awe.
4. **Tobious becomes a passenger.** The team must matter, but Tobious causes the failed rescue, chooses the bypass, refuses sacrifice, discloses his fault, accepts the new plan, honors Mariya's choice, saves the route, and completes the cut. Other competence should complicate his agency, not remove it.
5. **Mariya becomes the morally perfect community symbol.** Preserve concealed seed spending, false certainty, irritation, fatigue, physical desire, and a genuine attraction to another life. Let Nerissa and Orran change her decisions.
6. **Suss becomes a mouthpiece.** Show operational competence, care for specific people, and self-protective choices under pressure. One persuasive argument per encounter is stronger than a repeated manifesto.
7. **Too much grief without recovery.** Meals, humor, awe, rest, and small success must remain. The user asked for epic reading pleasure, not a continuous catalogue of suffering.
8. **Unearned skill.** Twelve weeks make Tobious a useful trained novice with unusual system access. He wins through preparation and cover, never suddenly surpasses a lifelong expert in a fair duel.
